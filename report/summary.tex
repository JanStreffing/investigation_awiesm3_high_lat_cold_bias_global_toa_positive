\begin{abstract}\noindent
The short reading of a campaign whose full record runs to 110 pages
(\texttt{report.tex}). It keeps what still constrains a decision and drops what died on
the way. Three objectives: the \textbf{global radiative imbalance}, the \textbf{Southern
Ocean}, and the \textbf{Siberian cold bias / boreal forest}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Adopt S4.} \texttt{RCL\_INPPMIN} $70000\to50000$, one namelist
number on the existing stack, is the best configuration the campaign has produced: it
takes the Southern Ocean surface shortwave bias from $+3.66$ to $-0.96$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ and
global net TOA from $+0.64$ to $\mathbf{+0.25}$ --- nearest the pre-industrial target of
anything run --- at \emph{no} cost in either Siberian season or the tropics. It finished on
2026--08--09 and sat unevaluated for two days while three more expensive arms were built.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The lever hunt beyond it is exhausted.} Rounds 26--28 built and
ran a marine-biogenic-CCN scheme, a temperature-selective cirrus lever, and a
cloud-overlap arm. All three work, none is adoptable: scored globally rather than in the
Southern Ocean box, they move the tropics as much as the Southern Ocean and are
\textbf{global dimming knobs wearing a regional label}. The one genuinely new physical
result --- \texttt{RSNOWLIN2}, which closes the entire tropical longwave deficit, five
times any previous lever --- \emph{warms} the Southern Ocean and worsens the global budget.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Siberia's two halves have separated.} Coupled 11E is the first
arm to warm Siberian summer without a winter penalty and is essentially the atmospheric
answer. The \emph{forest} is untouched: the coupled model inherits a real boreal forest
and destroys $92\,\%$ of the larch in fifty years. But offline 2000-year spin-ups now
calibrate the fix --- $+2$\,K of warming recovers $59\,\%$ of the forest gap, monotonically
and without saturating --- so closing the ${\approx}2$\,K cold bias should recover most of
it. The campaign's founding premise, measured rather than assumed.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Three structural problems surfaced} that no lever can address and
that change how results should be read: the AMIP runs are \textbf{not at 1850 forcing}
(Krakatoa is visible in them); the Southern Ocean's ``band mean'' and its ``RMSE'' are
\textbf{different fields} that disagree about which arm is best; and the coupled
amplification is \textbf{$1.40\times$ via sea-ice albedo, not cloud}, so an AMIP cloud
number is not what survives coupling. \S\ref{sec:forward} is what to do next.
\end{abstract}

Every 44-year arm scored on all three constraints at once --- Southern Ocean
\emph{surface} bias against CERES over ocean, the tropics, both Siberian seasons --- with
global net TOA carried alongside so that a global dimming knob cannot pass as a regional
fix:

LX1 is nominally closer to zero on the Southern Ocean and pays $0.83$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of

by dimming the planet. A1a damages the tropics \emph{and} Siberian summer, confirming its

Ocean while landing global net TOA near target and costing nothing elsewhere.}

\subsection{Why the new levers are global knobs}
Mapped globally rather than in the Southern Ocean box, LX3 moves the \textbf{tropics
$-4.05$} in TOA SW CRE against the Southern Ocean's $-5.61$ --- the same order, and
near-uniformly across every basin. It survives on \emph{net} only because
\texttt{RSNOWLIN2} adds $+2.46$ of global longwave cloud forcing in compensation: the
configuration makes the planet dimmer and more greenhouse-y and calls the residual a
Southern Ocean fix. DMS's seasonal selectivity at one year --- genuinely striking, $-7.25$
in DJF where the deficit is worst --- \textbf{does not become spatial selectivity at 44
years}.

\section{The global radiative imbalance}\label{sec:rad}

\subsection{The target is zero, and the reason is snow enthalpy}
The target was inherited as $-0.16$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, which was never derived --- it is
whatever an HR piControl happened to settle at. A target away from zero is only justified
if the atmosphere fails to conserve energy, because equilibrium requires
$\mathrm{SFC}=\mathrm{TOA}+S$ for a spurious atmospheric source $S$, so zero \emph{ocean}
drift would need $\mathrm{TOA}=-S$. Measured on 10A over its last decade, using the release
tool's own formula: net TOA $+0.854$, net surface $+0.877$, \textbf{residual
$-0.023$\,W\,m$^{-2}$}. The atmosphere conserves to $0.02$, so ``ocean not drifting'' and
``system not drifting'' are the same condition and both put the target at $\mathbf{0}$.

Snow enthalpy does nearly all of that reconciliation: $\mathtt{sf}\times3.3355\times10^{8}$
is $0.966$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, and omitting it makes the surface look like $+1.84$ against a TOA
of $+0.85$ --- the artefact that motivated $-0.16$-style fudges in the first place.

\subsection{Where it lives, and what is left}
Period-clean, the imbalance is not global but regional: the Southern Ocean band contributes
$+0.74$ of it on its own, through a $+7.46$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ band error. The tropics are
\emph{negative} --- $-0.67$ against CERES --- so they need more absorbed energy, not less.
That asymmetry is why a global brightness knob cannot fix the budget: it moves both bands
the same way and the two need opposite corrections.

S4 takes the AMIP imbalance to $+0.25$. Of the remaining $0.25$, roughly $0.15$ is the
transient-forcing artefact of \S\ref{sec:structural}, so the \emph{model's} residual
imbalance may be nearer $+0.1$ than $+0.25$. That cannot be confirmed without the
fixed-forcing control run.

\section{The tropical longwave: found, and unusable}\label{sec:troplw}

The four-way attribution splits the two remaining regional failures by channel: the
Southern Ocean fails through \textbf{shortwave} cloud ($+7.45$), the tropics through
\textbf{longwave} cloud ($-2.16$). For most of the campaign nothing moved the second at
all --- scored across all 50 archived arms, the largest positive response was A1a's
$+0.373$, $17\,\%$ of the deficit, and only as a by-product of a lever that wrecks
everything else. The term is extraordinarily stiff: interannual sd $0.101$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, so
the deficit is $51\times$ its own detection threshold.

\subsection{\texttt{RSNOWLIN2}: the largest tropical longwave response ever measured here}
\texttt{RSNOWLIN2} is the \emph{temperature exponent} of ice$\to$snow autoconversion,
$\mathrm{ZZCO}=\mathrm{PTSPHY}\cdot\mathtt{RSNOWLIN1}\cdot e^{\mathtt{RSNOWLIN2}(T-T_0)}$.
Raising it $0.030\to0.04$ slows the conversion $31\,\%$ at a $200$\,K cirrus temperature but
only $11\,\%$ at $250$\,K, so it builds \textbf{cold thin cirrus rather than thickening
anvils} --- and thin cirrus is longwave-dominant while a thick anvil is bright in both. The
contrast is measured: \texttt{DETRPEN} gives an LW:SW ratio of $0.44$, \texttt{RSNOWLIN2}
$1.17$.

Result: $+2.74$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of tropical LW CRE, \textbf{127\,\% of the deficit}, five times
anything else in the archive, with tropical and global net TOA both below threshold at one
year.

\subsection{Why it cannot be adopted}
At 44 years it adds $+0.61$ to Southern Ocean \emph{net} forcing --- its $+2.6$ of SO
longwave swamps its shortwave --- and $+0.42$ to global net TOA. On the bare model it was
neutral; on the adopted stack the sign flips. \textbf{The campaign's two remaining regional
targets are in direct tension through this one parameter}: it closes the tropical longwave
deficit partly by putting cirrus over the Southern Ocean, where the model already traps too
much.

The value $0.04$ is not invented --- it is the setting in the v3.4.2 TCO95L91-DARS2
runscripts, part of a jointly-tuned eight-parameter cloud stack for this exact resolution
that no \texttt{fort.4} on disk has ever executed. That stack was found only because
\texttt{preflight.py} was scanning one runtime tree and one runscript directory; it now
scans every one, and reports values rather than mentions.

\section{The Southern Ocean}\label{sec:so}

\subsection{Two fields, and they disagree}
The campaign quotes the Southern Ocean band mean from \textbf{TOA cloud radiative effect}
and its ``SW RMSE'' from \textbf{surface net shortwave over ocean}. These are different
quantities and they do not agree about which arm is best.\footnote{LX3 $=$ P5 $+$ \texttt{RSNOWLIN2}$=0.04$ $+$ DMS $+$ \texttt{RCL\_INPPMIN}$=50\,000$. Screened at 44 years and \emph{not} adopted --- it reaches the Southern Ocean by dimming the planet (\S\ref{sec:decision}). It is quoted here and in Fig.~\ref{fig:sostrip} only because it is the arm that separates the two fields most cleanly.}

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l r r}
\toprule
& TOA SW CRE bias & surface net SW bias \\
\midrule
control & $+7.69$ & $+3.66$ \\
P5 & $+4.98$ \emph{(bad)} & $+0.74$ \emph{(nearly right)} \\
LX3 & $-0.63$ \emph{(nearly right)} & $-4.75$ \emph{(bad)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Closing a TOA gap of ${\approx}5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ costs the surface ${\approx}5$, and it did,
almost exactly. \textbf{The two constraints cannot both be satisfied by adding cloud
reflection.} That is an \emph{atmospheric shortwave absorption} signature --- the model
would need cloud that reflects more at TOA without dimming the surface proportionally,
which is absorbing cloud, not more cloud. The surface error also exceeds the TOA error
\emph{globally} ($14.4$ against $11.5$), so this is not a Southern Ocean artefact. Until it
is resolved, the campaign has been optimising against whichever metric happened to be
quoted.

\subsection{What actually moves it, on NET}
Longwave cancels a very consistent $25$--$29\,\%$ of the shortwave gain across the whole
44-year archive, so $\mathrm{SO\,SW}\times0.72$ is a reliable rule for ordinary cloud
levers. Best net levers: A1a $-4.57$, D2a $-3.60$, S4 $-3.47$. The round-26/27 levers break
the rule --- \texttt{RSNOWLIN2} adds $+2.6$ of Southern Ocean \emph{longwave}, enough to
flip its net contribution positive on the adopted stack.

\subsection{The cloud-amount deficit is still open}
Two thirds of the band error is a ${\approx}6$\,pp cloud-\emph{amount} deficit. S4 adds
$+0.105$\,pp. Nothing in 58 runs has closed a meaningful fraction of it, and no identified
lever reaches it.

\section{The land surface: how the boreal half was actually solved}\label{sec:surface}

Twelve of the campaign's twenty-eight rounds were spent here, and this is where the largest
temperature gains of the campaign came from. The arc is worth stating compactly because the
adopted stack is almost entirely land.

\subsection{The bias is LPJ-GUESS, and it acts by two routes}
A purpose-built no-LPJG reference --- same CORE3 ocean, same TCO95 atmosphere, HTESSEL with
prescribed satellite vegetation, 30 coupled years --- differs from the baseline in
\textbf{LPJG alone}. Siberian high-vegetation cover is $0.63$ without and $\mathbf{0.02}$
with; the LPJG run is $\mathbf{-3.1}$\,K colder in DJF. Removing LPJG also \emph{improves}
global 2\,m-T RMSD ($1.514$ vs $1.717$) and CMPI ($0.738$ vs $0.745$) at a TOA imbalance
only $0.2$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ higher, so the warming is nearly free of the radiation objective.

The seasonal fingerprint identifies the mechanisms. The cold effect is present in every
month except high summer and peaks in the \emph{dark}: November $-3.9$\,K, April $-3.3$\,K,
July--August ${\approx}0$. A pure snow-albedo mechanism cannot do November. The second route
is \textbf{roughness}: needleleaf forest has $z_{0m}=2.0$\,m against $0.034$--$0.10$\,m for
the tundra and grass that replace it, and a smoother surface decouples from the atmosphere
in the stable boundary layer. The data carry exactly that signature --- a $+2.1$\,K stronger
$T_{925}-T_{2m}$ inversion, $+0.7$\,K stronger surface decoupling, $-4.7$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of
down-welling longwave, and \textbf{unchanged cloud} ($\Delta\approx0.00$, the negative
control). Spatially $r(\Delta\mathtt{cvh},\Delta\mathrm{inv})=-0.60$ against
$r(\Delta\mathtt{cvh},\Delta\mathrm{cloud})=-0.05$.

A residual survives with the forest intact: the model's winter inversion is $+1.4$\,K too
strong against ERA5, \emph{entirely surface-trapped} (the 925--1000\,hPa bias is $+0.07$),
and it explains $r=+0.76$ of where the model is too cold. Circulation ($r=0.27$) and cloud
fraction ($r=0.00$) are excluded. That is the IFS-family under-mixing stable boundary layer,
and its lever is orthogonal to everything tried --- \textbf{it remains unattacked}.

\subsection{Half the summer shortwave deficit is albedo, and it is melt timing}
Decomposed against CERES on the identical box and mask, the $12.5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ Siberian JJA
surface-SW deficit is $7.0$ cloud and $\mathbf{5.5}$ \textbf{surface albedo} --- and the
albedo half is almost entirely June ($13.4$ of it, against $1.7$ in July). Temperate and
tropical land is correct to $\pm0.004$, so the albedo scheme is sound; the error is
high-latitude ($+0.094$ on Siberian tundra) with \textbf{Fennoscandia, the same needleleaf
type, correct at $+0.002$}.

It is not a brightness error. Monthly, the model is too \emph{dark} in April ($-0.06$),
perfect when snow-free in July ($+0.004$), too bright in June ($+0.09$) and September
($+0.09$): \textbf{the snow season is too long at both ends}. Against ERA5 the accumulation
is right to 7--9\,\% with the monthly increments tracking almost exactly, and the whole
divergence is spring --- \textbf{the snowpack peaks a month late} (April still gaining
$+9.4$\,mm while ERA5 has begun melting) and \textbf{May melts $30\,\%$ too slowly} ($33.5$
against $47.5$\,mm). Satellite confirms it independently: melt-out 13 days late.

That closes a runaway. In May the \emph{downwelling} flux is nearly right ($-3.5$) while the
net is $-13.9$: pure albedo. Late melt $\to$ more May snow $\to$ higher albedo $\to$ less
absorbed $\to$ less melt energy $\to$ snow survives into June. The model melts slower
\emph{despite} having darker snow than ERA5 --- the brightness term works against the bias
and the extent term wins.

Two cause-and-effect checks kept this honest. February--April is simultaneously too cold
\emph{and} too dark, so the winter cold is \textbf{not} albedo-driven. And insolation-weighted
over global land, the albedo-induced SW loss correlates with the T2m bias at only $r=+0.145$
--- cells where the model is too \emph{dark} are still $-1.16$\,K cold. \textbf{Albedo is
worth fixing and is not the root cause.}

\noindent Three things in that table matter more than the numbers. \textbf{F4 was anchored on
an observation, not a sweep} --- the modelled Siberian JJA Bowen ratio is $0.43$ against
$0.5$--$1.5$ observed --- and it moved Bowen to $0.60$, into the observed range without
overshooting, while costing the tropics nothing ($42.68$ against $42.61$). \textbf{The
strongest lever was disqualified by a season nobody was scoring}: B5, the previous best,
carried a hidden DJF $-0.72$\,K, invisible for the whole campaign because only JJA was
evaluated. DJF has been a standing guardrail since. And \textbf{K1's negative result was
worth more than its $+0.089$\,K}: it removed $74\,\%$ of a directly measured global land
albedo bias and bought a sixth of the target, which \emph{closes out land albedo} as the
explanation for the universal cold bias --- at a measured sensitivity of $0.116$\,K per
W\,m$^{-2}$ against $0.12$ assumed.

\subsection{The snow scheme, rebuilt from observations}
Route A --- transpiration to cloud --- is closed: the vegetation levers act through surface
exchange, not cloud. Route B --- snow to albedo --- is where the gains came from, and it
required replacing the scheme outright. The inherited \texttt{tanh} depletion has range
$(0,1)$ \emph{open at 1}, so complete snow cover is not representable at any parameter
setting, while RIHMI-WDC snow courses report exactly 10/10 in $99.74\,\%$ of 14\,369
Siberian DJF surveys. Against 174\,676 station observations the \texttt{tanh} scored $-26.3$
against an as-released $-5.3$; coupled, it drove the soil $\mathbf{22.7}$\,K cold and
collapsed the soil-to-snow January gradient from $22.4$\,K to $1.8$\,K, which is physically
impossible. \texttt{ECE\_SNOW\_SCF}$=3$ replaces it with $\min(1,(d/d_c)^b)$, $d_c$ split by
vegetation type and fitted to 36\,492 snow-course surveys, with \texttt{SWEMIN} flooring
$d_c$ at a minimum snow \emph{mass} --- the right currency, since the failure it fixes is a
heat-capacity collapse when a sub-millimetre dusting is given full cover.

It passed both pre-registered falsifiers and beats the scheme-off baseline on every soil
metric (bias $-1.13$ against $-1.52$, RMSE $3.12$ against $3.28$), and the winter penalty
that had followed every snow lever since round 15 is gone. \textbf{It also costs the
autumn}: the fitted $d_c$ for fresh low-density snow is ${\sim}2$\,mm, correct at the
1--2\,km scale the snow courses measure and wrong at grid scale, so September Rutgers cover
goes $2.4\times$ worse ($+0.259$ against $+0.107$). The offline sweep puts the whole-season
optimum at $\mathtt{SWEMIN}=30$, but coupled and in AMIP $15$ wins --- the offline score
holds the albedo$\to$melt$\to$SWE feedback fixed, and the feedback is the point.

\textbf{The single most useful thing the land work produced is an observational anchor.} 105
RIHMI-WDC stations of Siberian soil temperature, QC-filtered, turned two rounds of
plausible-looking snow tuning into a decided question in one figure.

\subsection{The ceiling}
Boreal screen temperature is remarkably insensitive to surface shortwave: $0.02$--$0.10$\,K
per W\,m$^{-2}$ across the radiative levers, and one manages the wrong sign. Closing the
\emph{entire} $14$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ deficit would buy ${\approx}1.3$\,K of a ${\approx}2$\,K
bias. The energy goes to sensible heat and the temperature hardly responds. \textbf{The
cloud error was never the whole story}: the land levers stop at half the bias, and the
residual sits in the global tropospheric cold bias that no land lever reaches.

\section{Siberia and the boreal forest}\label{sec:sib}

\subsection{The atmospheric half is done}
Coupled \textbf{11E} (11D at \texttt{SWEMIN}$=15$ plus K1) is the first coupled arm to warm
Siberian summer \emph{without} a winter penalty --- JJA $+0.73$, DJF $+0.39$, DJF soil
$+0.39$ against the baseline, where every previous gain came with a winter cost. Against
11D on a common 30-year window it gives DJF soil $\mathbf{+0.90}$\,K, resolved against a
$\pm0.37$ threshold: \textbf{the AMIP choice of \texttt{SWEMIN}$=15$ transfers to the
coupled model}, as predicted from P5-versus-P6.

A caution that cost a wrong sign: at a 10-year window the 11E$-$11D DJF difference came out
$-0.54$; at 30 years it is $+0.40$. Coupled comparisons need $\geq$30 years.

\subsection{The forest is not}
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r}
\toprule
Siberian box, 473 cells & BNS (larch) & TREE & tree$>$0.5 & grass$>$tree \\
\midrule

11E at year 1350 (start) & $0.213$ & $0.394$ & $47.8\,\%$ & $28.3\,\%$ \\
\textbf{11E after 50 coupled years} & $\mathbf{0.017}$ & $\mathbf{0.083}$ & $2.1\,\%$ & $\mathbf{69.6\,\%}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\noindent \textbf{The coupled model inherits a real boreal forest and dismantles it in
fifty years} --- $92\,\%$ of the larch --- from a healthy state, while clearing the larch
growing-degree gate on $91\,\%$ of cells. It is a transient toward a much poorer
equilibrium, not an endpoint: forest composition takes centuries.

\section{Southern Ocean and Siberia are not in competition}\label{sec:both}

Across the coupled archive the two original faults appeared to trade off almost linearly:
every W\,m$^{-2}$ of TOA improvement cost ${\approx}0.6$\,K of global cold. \textbf{That
line is a property of one lever family, not of the model.} It was drawn by aggressive ocean
mixing, which lowers TOA by cooling the planet --- necessarily coupling the two objectives.

Levers acting on disjoint geography and disjoint physics escape it. D2b (Southern Ocean ice
nucleation) and F4/G4 (boreal surface exchange) superpose additively to within noise on
every metric, and S4 --- the arm now adopted --- improves the Southern Ocean surface with
Siberian JJA $+1.03$ and DJF $+0.05$, i.e. no interaction at all.

The round-26/27 levers are the counter-example that proves the rule: they are \emph{not}
geographically disjoint, and they behave exactly as the ocean-mixing family did, trading
one objective against another. \textbf{Selectivity is a property to be verified globally,
not assumed from the mechanism} --- DMS looked seasonally selective at one year and turned
out to be a global knob at 44.

\section{The shared limiter: the tropopause and the cold point}\label{sec:limiter}

Both remaining objectives run into the same upper-atmosphere error, and it is not what it
first looked like.

\dead{The extratropical cold-and-dry pair is a displaced tropopause.} The WMO lapse-rate
tropopause sits on the \emph{same level} as ERA5 in all five latitude bands. What is there
instead is a \emph{sharper transition}: the model cools $+1.8$\,K\,km$^{-1}$ faster than
ERA5 through 300--250\,hPa at the poles and $1.5$--$1.9$ slower above 200.

And the cold and dry halves are \textbf{one error, not two}. Stratospheric water vapour is
set by the temperature of the tropical cold point, which freeze-dries air entering the
stratosphere; the Brewer--Dobson circulation then carries it poleward. The model's tropical
cold point is $193.44$\,K against ERA5's $195.51$ --- $-2.07$\,K --- and Clausius--Clapeyron
over ice predicts $-28.5\,\%$ of entry-air drying against a measured $-46.9\,\%$ at
100\,hPa. \textbf{A $2$\,K cold-point bias accounts for three fifths of a $47\,\%$
stratospheric dry bias.}

That is much better news than the alternative. Two independent cancelling errors would have
to be fixed together or not at all, and fixing either alone moves net TOA several
W\,m$^{-2}$ the wrong way. One error with one entry point means \textbf{a lever that warms
the tropical cold point should move both}, in the right direction, at once. No such lever
has been identified, and the remaining $39\,\%$ --- transport, or UTLS microphysics --- is
untraced.

\emph{Caveat carried forward:} ERA5's upper-level humidity is weakly observationally
constrained, so the $-46.9\,\%$ denominator is the softest number here. The $-2.07$\,K cold
point rests only on temperature, which ERA5 constrains well.

\section{The marine-CCN line: what was built, and why it is shelved}\label{sec:dms}

Two rounds went into this and it produced real physics, so it is documented rather than
merely dismissed.

\textbf{What the model was missing.} The aerosol--cloud path was traced end to end. Genthon
(1992) supplies sea-salt CCN from wind and MACv2-SP supplies anthropogenic plumes; there
was \textbf{no marine biogenic CCN anywhere}. The consequence was measurable: Southern
Ocean droplet number is right in the \emph{mean} ($N_d\approx73$\,cm$^{-3}$ against an
observed 60--120) but its \textbf{seasonal phase is inverted} --- lowest in DJF ($68.5$),
highest in JJA ($76.1$), where observations peak in DJF with the bloom and Cape Grim sees a
2--3$\times$ summer/winter ratio.

\textbf{What was built.} DMS-Rev3 (Hulswar et al.\ 2022) was ingested through ocp-tool onto
the reduced Gaussian grid as an eighth ICMCL field --- zero holes at TCO95 --- and wired
into \texttt{radpar.F90} after \texttt{SURFRAD} fills \texttt{PCCNO}: Saltzman et al.\
(1993) Schmidt number, Nightingale et al.\ (2000) transfer velocity, $F=kC_w$, and
$\mathtt{PCCNO}\mathrel{+}=S\cdot F_S$ with $S$ a namelist knob defaulting to zero. It
flows through the Martin et al.\ (1994) quadratic to droplet number and effective radius
--- the Twomey effect. Everything is gated so that with the switch off the configuration
reproduces \textbf{bit-for-bit}, which was verified.

\textbf{What it showed.} Woodhouse et al.\ (2010) measure $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}F$ from
$-43$ to $+166$\,cm$^{-3}$ per mg\,S\,m$^{-2}$d$^{-1}$, too wide to pick from, so the range
was bracketed. At the top of it:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r r}
\toprule
$S=166$, SW CRE & DJF & MAM & JJA & SON & annual \\
\midrule
SO 65--45S & $\mathbf{-7.25}$ & $+0.03$ & $-0.63$ & $-1.14$ & $-2.25$ \\
tropics & $-1.56$ & $-2.17$ & $-2.44$ & $-2.39$ & $-2.14$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The DJF response is $67\,\%$ of the $+10.86$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ austral-summer deficit,
in the season the deficit is worst, and \textbf{the selectivity is the bloom's} --- no
hemispheric constant was invented to produce it. DJF is austral summer and carries the
shortwave: incoming solar there is $460$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ against $87$ in JJA.

\textbf{Why it is shelved.} The tropical cost is $0.0129$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ per unit $S$, so the
$\pm0.5$ tolerance binds at $S\approx39$ --- below Woodhouse's published floor of 43. And
at 44 years in combination the seasonal selectivity does not survive: the tropics move
almost as much as the Southern Ocean. \textbf{No coefficient in the published range works
as a tuning knob.} The code and the input stay in the model, inert.

\textbf{One coupled result is worth keeping}: 11F, cancelled at 20 of 50 years, is the only
coupled run that ever reached the energy goal ($+0.017$ over its decade) and it supplied
the $1.40\times$ amplification factor of \S\ref{sec:amp}. Both are decade-offset from the
rest of the family and must not be read as production numbers.

\section{Coupled amplification: $1.40\times$, and it is sea ice}\label{sec:amp}

11F minus 11E over their common 20 years is a clean coupled pair differing only by the DMS
coefficient --- the same lever measured in AMIP as U1 minus T3. Coupled SO net TOA moved
$-2.767$ against AMIP's $-1.976$: \textbf{amplification $1.40\times$}, with SO SST
$-0.598$\,K and sea-ice fraction $+3.2$\,pp, all resolved.

\textbf{The mechanism matters more than the factor.} The coupled \emph{cloud} response was
\emph{smaller} than AMIP's ($-0.44$ against $-2.25$, not even resolved) because the cloud
adjusts away once the surface cools. The amplification comes from \textbf{sea-ice albedo,
which is clear-sky, not cloud forcing}. So a lever's AMIP cloud-radiative-effect number is
\emph{not} what survives coupling --- the surface response is. This is also why S4, which
acts on the \emph{surface}, is the right candidate to test coupled, and why 20 years makes
$1.40$ a lower bound: sea ice adjusts on decadal timescales.

\section{Three structural problems}\label{sec:structural}

\subsection{The AMIP runs are not at 1850 forcing}
\texttt{NCMIPFIXYR=1850} sits in \texttt{\&NAMECECMIP6} --- the block 48r1 renamed and no
longer reads. The runtime value is $-1$ and the model reads \emph{yearly} GHG, ozone and
aerosol: the log shows 1869 in the first leg and 1911 in the last. \textbf{Krakatoa is
visible} as a synchronised 1883--86 dip in every AMIP arm (Fig.~\ref{fig:nettoa}) --- a run
at fixed 1850 forcing cannot contain a volcano.

Every \emph{lever delta} stands, because both arms of every pair share the forcing. The
\emph{absolute} energy baseline does not: mean CO$_2$ over the window is ${\approx}294$\,ppm
against a PI $284.3$, worth ${\approx}+0.15$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, about a quarter of the control's
$+0.64$. The coupled runs \emph{are} correctly pinned. A one-line fix in the live namelist
was verified to work (run Z1) and is deliberately \emph{not} applied to runs in flight,
because it would break comparability with the whole archive; it needs one dedicated
control run instead.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{campaign_net_toa_by_year_amip.png}
\caption{\textbf{AMIP net TOA by year.} The synchronised 1883--86 dip is Krakatoa. S4 lands
nearest the pre-industrial goal; LX3 and LY2 overshoot past it; \texttt{RSNOWLIN2} alone

\end{figure}

\subsection{TOA and surface disagree}
Covered in \S\ref{sec:so}: the surface shortwave error exceeds the TOA error everywhere,
and no cloud-reflectivity lever can satisfy both. This is the most likely reason the
Southern Ocean has resisted 58 runs, and it is one pass over existing data to test ---
atmospheric absorption is TOA net SW minus surface net SW, model against CERES.

\subsection{The error field is dominated by things no lever touches}
Global RMSE barely moves between arms (TOA $11.48\to12.40$, surface $14.44\to15.18$)
because the pattern is set by the tropical Pacific dipole, the stratocumulus decks off
Peru, Namibia and California, and the land signals --- all at $\pm20$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ and all
unchanged by everything tried. Against those, \textbf{the Southern Ocean box is
comparatively well-behaved}.

\section{Where the energy error actually sits}\label{sec:decomp}

The global imbalance is small because large regional errors cancel. Period-clean, per band,
model minus CERES:

band & net TOA error & channel \\
\midrule
Southern Ocean 45--65S & $+7.46$ & \textbf{shortwave cloud} --- too little reflection \\
tropics 30S--30N & $-0.67$ & \textbf{longwave cloud} --- too little trapping ($-2.16$ in LW CRE) \\

\noindent \textbf{The two regional failures act through different channels}, which is why

not regionally selective, so the gate was never the binding constraint. What binds is
selectivity itself.

\subsection{The tropics are too dark, not too bright}
Worth stating explicitly because it has caused sign errors, including in this campaign's
own scoring: the tropics sit \emph{below} CERES. They need \emph{more} absorbed energy. A
lever that adds tropical reflection is spending an objective, not improving one; a lever
that adds tropical absorption is helping. The $\pm0.5$ tolerance is therefore
\textbf{one-sided}.

\section{The path forward}\label{sec:forward}

Cheapest-decisive first.

\textbf{1. 11G --- S4 coupled} (submitted, job 26868368). 11E with one namelist line
changed, a strict one-variable pair against a completed 50-year run. Tests the arm that
now looks best on both headline targets, and the $1.40\times$ sea-ice amplification should
make its coupled effect \emph{larger} than the AMIP number. Score on a $\geq$30-year window.

\textbf{2. Atmospheric shortwave absorption.} One pass over existing data. If the model
absorbs the wrong amount of shortwave \emph{within} the atmosphere, then the Southern Ocean
cannot be fixed by cloud reflectivity at all, and every remaining cloud lever is chasing
the wrong error. This is the highest-information item on the list and costs no compute.

\textbf{3. One fixed-forcing control run} to re-baseline the absolute energy target.
Do \emph{not} mix fixed- and transient-forcing arms in one comparison; the archive's deltas
transfer, only the baseline needs re-establishing.

\textbf{4. The final land round.} It now has what it never had: the $+1/+2$\,K transfer
function, three unread competition experiments, and the knowledge that 50 coupled years
shows a transient rather than an equilibrium, so vegetation must be judged offline or over
centuries.

\textbf{Do not} spend more runs on: the snow scheme (exhausted), marine CCN as a tuning
knob (no published coefficient works), \texttt{RCL\_OVERLAPLIQICE} (damages the boreal at
$0.10$, negligible at $0.35$), or single-lever Southern Ocean cloud hunts until item 2 is
answered.

\section{What would change the picture}\label{sec:falsifiers}

Stated in advance, so the next round can be scored against them rather than narrated.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{11G improves coupled net TOA and cools SO SST.} Then S4 is adopted for the
spin-up and the Southern Ocean objective is as closed as this campaign can make it.

after all --- through the ice, not through the cloud.

informative thing left and it costs no compute.

forest can be recovered independently of the cold bias, and the land round is a vegetation
problem rather than an atmospheric one.

quarter of the ``remaining'' imbalance was never model error, and S4's $+0.25$ is closer to
$+0.1$.
