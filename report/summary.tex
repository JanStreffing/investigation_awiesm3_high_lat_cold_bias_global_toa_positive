\begin{abstract}\noindent
This is the short reading of a campaign whose full record runs to eighty-six pages
(\texttt{report.tex}). It keeps the results that still constrain a decision and drops the
mechanisms that died on the way. Three objectives are tracked --- the \textbf{global radiative
imbalance}, the \textbf{Southern Ocean}, and the \textbf{Siberian cold bias} --- and the
campaign's central operational finding is about the relationship between them.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The headline.} Across 29 coupled runs the two original faults trade
off almost perfectly linearly: every W\,m$^{-2}$ of TOA improvement has cost ${\approx}0.6$\,K
of global cold. That line is \emph{not} a property of the model. It is a property of the one
lever family that was used to buy the imbalance --- aggressive ocean mixing, which lowers TOA by
cooling the planet. The regime-selective levers built later escape it: D2b (Southern-Ocean ice
nucleation) and F4/G4 (boreal surface exchange) act on disjoint geography and disjoint physics,
and when combined they superpose additively to within noise on every metric. \textbf{Southern
Ocean and Siberia are not in competition; the ocean-mixing stack was the competition.}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Where we are.} \textbf{K1 $+$ P5} is carried forward: Siberian JJA
$+1.15$\,K with every season inside its own noise threshold, on a snow scheme rebuilt from
36\,492 Russian snow-course surveys after the original was falsified outright. Coupled 10A sits
at net TOA $+0.85$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ against a goal of $0$, of which only $0.18$ is still reachable
from the ocean side. The Southern Ocean carries $+5.81$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of band error from a
$6.6$\,pp cloud-\emph{area} deficit that no lever has yet moved by more than a fraction.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What to do next} is \S\ref{sec:forward}: attack the SO cloud-area
deficit rather than escalating ocean mixing; stop optimising the snow scheme; and diagnose the
global tropospheric cold bias, which is now the shared limiter on both remaining objectives and
has never been worked on in 50 runs.
\end{abstract}

\section{The global radiative imbalance}\label{sec:rad}

\subsection{The target is zero, and the reason is snow}
The inherited goal of $-0.16$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ was never derived; it is whatever the HR piControl
settled at. A target displaced from zero is defensible only if the atmosphere fails to conserve
energy: with a spurious atmospheric source $S$, equilibrium gives $\mathrm{SFC}=\mathrm{TOA}+S$,
so zero \emph{ocean} drift would require $\mathrm{TOA}=-S$. Measured on 10A over 1390--1399:

\noindent The atmosphere conserves to $0.02$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, so ``the ocean is not drifting'' and
``the system is not drifting'' are the same condition and both put the goal at $\mathbf{0}$.
\textbf{Snow enthalpy does nearly all the reconciliation} --- $0.97$ of a $0.99$ discrepancy.
Omit it and the surface reads $+1.84$ against a TOA of $+0.85$; that artefact is exactly the
kind of apparent leak an offset target gets invented to absorb. Anyone comparing a raw surface
flux against TOA without it will see a spurious ${\approx}1$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ gap.

\section{The Southern Ocean}\label{sec:so}

\subsection{It is a cloud deficit, and it is atmospheric}
The SO band's $+5.81$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ is not subtle in origin: cloud area $83.1\,\%$ against CERES's
$89.7\,\%$ ($-6.6$\,pp), SW CRE $-60.3$ against $-68.1$ ($+7.8$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ too little
reflection). Measured in AMIP, \emph{with SST and sea ice prescribed to observations}, so it
cannot be an ocean-state error. \textbf{This is precisely the Hyder et al.\ (2018) signature} ---
the leading 40--60\textdegree S warm bias traced to atmospheric net-surface-flux errors dominated
by shortwave and cloud.

The measurement is also period-clean: the present-day arm gives an SO SW CRE gap of $+7.78$
against the PI arm's $+7.85$, an epoch offset of only $-0.07$\,W\,m$^{-2}$. Whatever this is,
it is the same in both epochs.

\subsection{The compensating-error problem, stated plainly}
Part~I of the campaign bought most of its imbalance reduction from ocean mixing --- 1-hourly
coupling, MOMIX, weaker KPP --- which suppresses spurious Weddell/Ross open-ocean winter
convection, thickens the pack, and removes the SO surface warm bias. Damping that convection is
physically the right direction: it is the canonical CMIP Antarctic bias, and models over-express
a real-but-rare mode. But three independent lines now say the lever is partly compensating for
an atmospheric error:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Our own AMIP measurement.} The SO carries $+5.81$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ with the ocean held
at observations. That component cannot be fixed from the ocean side, and an ocean lever that
removes the resulting surface warmth is cancelling a symptom.
\item \textbf{The CMPI region map.} 06O/06T improve \texttt{thetao}, \texttt{so} and
\texttt{siconc} --- exactly where ocean mixing should act --- but degrade
\texttt{tas}/\texttt{zg}/\texttt{rlut} by $+0.16$ to $+0.26$ and redden \emph{every} region
including the tropics and Ni\~no-3.4. Cooling leaking to where the parameter has no business
acting is the diagnostic signature of a compensating error.
\item \textbf{The price.} $0.6$\,K of global cold per W\,m$^{-2}$ (\S\ref{sec:both}).
\end{itemize}

\includegraphics[width=0.76\textwidth]{plots/mld_winter_antarctic.png}
\caption{Antarctic September mixed-layer depth: Baseline absolute (top-left, own scale), others

\subsection{What has actually moved it, and what has been excluded}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{D2b (ice nuclei over sea, gated below 700\,hPa) is adopted.} Best SO surface-SW
RMSE of any run, $6.88\to4.96$ ($-28\,\%$), at \emph{zero} tropical cost ($+0.09$). It beats the
ungated D2a despite a smaller mean-CRE change, because it improves the spatial \emph{pattern}.
The pressure gate did exactly what it was designed to do: D2a's tropical cost vanishes while
$55\,\%$ of the SO gain is retained.
\item \textbf{Independent support for the mechanism.} SO relative humidity is $+5.8$ to
$+6.6\,\%$ too high through the mixed-phase layer \emph{while} reflecting too little shortwave
--- humidity present, liquid absent, which is the supercooled-liquid deficit an INP error
predicts.
\item \textbf{Aerosol is excluded, by measurement.} It had been \emph{inferred} at
${\sim}0.7$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ (13\,\% of the SO clear-sky excess) from a $+0.033$ AOD excess against
MISR. Removing \emph{all} anthropogenic MACv2-SP plumes moves SO clear-sky by
$\mathbf{-0.116}$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ ($t=-18.9$, paired over 25 years) --- \textbf{six times smaller},
${\sim}2\,\%$ not 13\,\%. The unexplained residual therefore \emph{grew}, from ${\sim}1.3$ to
${\sim}1.9$\,W\,m$^{-2}$. A related trap: M1's effect is overwhelmingly on \emph{cloud}
($-0.54$ global against $+0.13$ clear-sky, via \texttt{LMACV2SP\_CCNF}), so any future aerosol
argument scored on the clear-sky column alone is invalid.
\item \textbf{The 60--90S band is the most responsive in the model}, moving $-0.4$ to $-3.3$
under levers designed for entirely different purposes. It is not inert; it has simply never been
targeted. The G-series buys about $-2.0$ of the $+8.4$ needed there, all of it from D2b, as a
by-product.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{Net: roughly a quarter of the Southern Ocean gap is closed, all of it
incidentally, and the ${\sim}6$\,pp cloud-area deficit remains untouched by all 34 evaluated
levers.} That is the single largest unexploited term in the campaign.

\section{Siberia}\label{sec:sib}

\subsection{The bias, and its two routes}
The period-clean Siberian JJA cold bias is $-2.04$\,K (measured directly against the model's own
present-day arm, not chained through observed epoch change --- the earlier indirect estimate was
wrong by $2.7\times$). AMIP alone reproduces essentially all of the coupled model's summer bias
($-2.16$ against 09A's $-2.19$), so \textbf{the summer half is atmospheric}; the cold-season half
is not, and belongs to the coupled vegetation problem.

Two routes were identified and both are now resolved:

\textbf{Route A --- transpiration to cloud: closed.} Siberian JJA cloud fraction was $78.1\,\%$
against CERES's $69.6\,\%$, carrying $-7.04$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of the surface SW deficit. Raising
minimum canopy resistance on boreal types (F4, then G4 adding tundra) moves the cloud term
$-7.04\to+1.20$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, worth $+0.952$\,K, with the tropics untouched ($+0.12$). Bowen
ratio moves $0.44\to0.60$, into the observed range without overshooting. \emph{Caveat kept:}
\texttt{RVRSMIN} $250\to1000$ is an unanchored $4\times$ excursion from what ECMWF ships, and it
does not saturate --- $500/1000/2000$ give $+0.336/+0.521/+0.876$, so $1000$ is a cap set by
winter damage, not a knee.

\textbf{Route B --- snow to albedo: real, measured, and worth far less than it looked.} June
surface albedo is $+0.046$ too high against CERES, June snow cover $0.380$ against a satellite
$0.203$, melt-out 13 days late. The cause was found: HTESSEL has \emph{no} sub-grid snow
depletion, so cover stays pinned near 1 while the median cell still holds 27\,cm in May. A
depletion scheme was built, and that took five rounds to get right (\S\ref{sec:sib_snow}). The
honest verdict on the whole line is $+0.1$ to $+0.3$\,K of Siberian JJA, not the ${\approx}1$\,K
the early rounds implied.

\subsection{The snow scheme, in one paragraph}\label{sec:sib_snow}
The first formulation used $\tanh$. Its range is open at $1$, so complete snow cover is
\emph{unrepresentable at any parameters} --- while Russian snow courses report exactly $10/10$ in
$99.74\,\%$ of 14\,369 Siberian DJF surveys. Cost, against 174\,676 station soil observations:
$-20.2$\,K, where the scheme \emph{off} is right to $+0.8$\,K. The damage was distributional, not
a matter of mean cover: DJF cover is identical to $0.002$ across the variants while the soil
differs by 23\,K, because a clipping ramp leaves $93.5\,\%$ of the box perfectly insulated and
the $\tanh$ smears the same deficit into every cell. It was rebuilt as
$\mathrm{SCF}=(1-c_{vh})\min(1,(d/d_{cl})^{b_l})+c_{vh}\min(1,(d/d_{ch})^{b_h})$ with
$d_c\propto(\rho/\rho_{ref})^{4.7}$ --- density, a prognostic the model already carries, as the
age/melt proxy, so the scheme works in a 10\,\textdegree C warmer world or an ice age --- fitted
to 36\,492 snow-course surveys, then floored by a minimum snow \emph{mass} \texttt{SWEMIN} after
the literal fit gave full cover to a $0.56$\,mm dusting and aborted the run. \textbf{P5
(\texttt{SWEMIN}$=15$) is adopted}: every season inside its own threshold, and the autumn cover
excess the fit introduced cut by $40\,\%$ against Rutgers.

\subsection{The ceiling, and what it exposes}
Marginal over K1 against the $\pm0.242$ threshold: P3 $+0.305$ (marginal), P5 $+0.113$ (noise),
P6 $+0.060$ (noise). The reason is structural. Against Rutgers' observed May cover of $0.655$,
the as-released model reads $0.773$ --- \textbf{the entire available error is $0.118$}, P5
removes $0.044$, and a perfect correction would be worth roughly $+0.2$\,K. The $\tanh$'s larger
apparent gain was never legitimate: it took $0.158$ out of a $0.118$ error, overshooting into a
$-0.040$ deficit, so any comparison against its $+1.350$ is a comparison against an error.
\textbf{Further \texttt{SWEMIN} optimisation is not worth model time.}

\subsection{Closed by measurement}
Three further Siberian routes are shut, which matters because each was once the leading
candidate. \textbf{Land surface albedo} is not the explanation for the universal bias: removing
$74\,\%$ of a directly measured excess bought $0.089$\,K of ${\sim}0.7$\,K, so a perfect fix buys
${\sim}0.12$\,K, one sixth of the target. \textbf{Skin conductivity} is falsified twice over ---
raising exposed-snow $\lambda_{sk}$ made DJF monotonically \emph{worse} ($-2.834$ at $15$,
$-3.226$ at $25$), and the moss/organic-layer proxy was worse still ($-0.168$ and $-0.314$\,K vs
G4). \textbf{The boreal budget is nearly spent}: Siberia's $-2.58$\,K JJA is ${\sim}0.7$
universal (present over prescribed-SST ocean too), ${\sim}0.3$ orographic and $1.3$--$1.6$
boreal-specific, and G4 has taken ${\sim}0.95$ of the last. The remainder is not reachable by a
boreal-indexed lever --- which is why F5, combining four boreal levers, equals F4 alone to
$0.003$\,K.

\section{Why the Southern Ocean and Siberia can both fit}\label{sec:both}

\subsection{The trade-off line is a property of one lever family}
\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{plots/summary_scatter_with0910_picorr.png}
\caption{Net TOA imbalance against 2\,m-T bias vs ERA5, \emph{corrected for the reference
period} (ERA5 is present-day, the runs pre-industrial, ${\approx}1.1$\,K apart, so $0$ is correct
for a PI run). \textbf{The runs lie on a line, not in a cloud}: from 080a at $(1.73,+0.28)$ to
10A at $(0.57,-0.93)$, every W\,m$^{-2}$ of TOA improvement has cost ${\approx}0.6$\,K of global
cold. \textbf{And the spatial pattern is not what distinguishes them} --- centred RMSD,
$\sqrt{\mathrm{RMSD}^2-\mathrm{bias}^2}$, is $1.41$--$1.64$\,K across all 29 runs, a $\pm8\,\%$
spread against a raw-RMSD spread of $64\,\%$. The entire apparent skill difference between these
runs is one number: the global mean.}
\end{figure}

Two facts about that figure decide the strategy.

\textbf{(i) The slope belongs to the ocean-mixing family, not to the model.} Every point on the
steep part of the line is a coupling/mixing variant. Those levers lower TOA by cooling the
planet, which is why they trace a line rather than a cloud, and why CMPI degrades everywhere
including regions an ocean-mixing parameter has no business reaching.

\textbf{(ii) The pattern error is flat.} If the runs differed in \emph{where} they are wrong, the
centred RMSD would spread; it does not. So the coupled ``cold bias'' objective is a global-mean
problem, and a lever that fixes a regional radiative error without changing the global mean
temperature moves the $x$ axis \emph{without} moving the $y$ axis. That is a corner of the plot
nothing currently occupies, and it is reachable.

\subsection{Disjoint levers superpose --- measured, not assumed}
G1 $=$ F4 (boreal surface exchange) $+$ D2b (SO ice nucleation) was the first pairing whose
components are disjoint in both process and geography. Because two earlier combinations had got
superposition wrong \emph{in sign}, it was measured rather than assumed:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r l}
\toprule
metric & predicted (sum) & measured & \\
\midrule
Siberia JJA T2m [K] & $+0.527$ & $\mathbf{+0.521}$ & additive \\
SO surface-SW RMSE & $-2.131$ & $-2.067$ & additive \\
SO TOA SW CRE [W\,m$^{-2}$] & $-2.719$ & $-2.840$ & additive \\
global net TOA [W\,m$^{-2}$] & $-0.188$ & $-0.195$ & additive \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Additive to within noise on every metric. The plausible reason it holds here and failed
for the earlier pairs: F4 acts on a land surface flux in boreal summer, D2b on ice nucleation
over ocean below 700\,hPa, whereas the failed combinations modified the \emph{same} cloud scheme
in the \emph{same} regime.

\subsection{The recipe}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Buy the imbalance with Southern-Ocean cloud, not with global cooling.} The SO
deficit is a reflection error over ocean; correcting it adds reflected SW where the model is
warm-biased. It lowers TOA \emph{and} cools a region that should be cooler, so it moves both
axes the right way instead of trading them.
\item \textbf{Buy Siberia with boreal-indexed surface exchange and the snow scheme.} Both are
land-only, summer-weighted, and by construction cannot touch the tropics or the ocean.
\item \textbf{Stop escalating ocean mixing.} There is only $0.18$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of coupled-state
term left in 10A anyway, and buying it costs ${\approx}0.1$\,K of global cold for a term the
atmosphere is now known to dominate 4:1.
\item \textbf{Score every lever regionally.} The per-band TOA decomposition existed only for the
control until recently --- the energy target was tracked by one global number with no idea which
band any lever was moving. It is now in the standard table for every run, and it is what makes
the disjointness in (1)--(2) verifiable rather than asserted.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The G-series is the existence proof: G1/G4 improves the Southern Ocean SW RMSE by
$30\,\%$, warms Siberian JJA by ${\approx}1$\,K, leaves the tropics inside $+0.12$, and lowers
global net TOA. That combination is off the trade-off line.

\noindent This separates two problems that had been conflated for most of the campaign. The
boreal-specific excess lives entirely in the bottom three levels --- the signature a
surface-exchange problem should have, which is what validates the F/G framing. \textbf{Above
700\,hPa Siberia merely shares a global tropospheric cold bias of $0.7$ to $2.2$\,K that no
boreal lever will ever touch}, and which has never been worked on in 50 runs.

Three separate observations now converge on it, which is why it belongs in a summary rather than
an appendix:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item It is the residual in the Siberian budget --- the ${\sim}0.7$\,K ``universal'' component
present over prescribed-SST ocean and under every one of the 20 surface types.
\item It is what the RIHMI station network sees as the $2$--$3.5$\,\textdegree C April--September
soil deficit, identical in every AMIP run including scheme-off.
\item It is the natural suspect for the residual radiative error. A cold troposphere under-emits;
the model's OLR is $-0.74$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ below CERES while its absorbed SW is only $-0.37$ below,
so relative to observation the model's positive imbalance \emph{is} its OLR deficit.
\end{itemize}

\noindent That last point needs a caveat and it is worth stating precisely, because it is the
next thing to test. A Planck scaling of a ${\approx}1.5$\,K cold emitting layer predicts an OLR
deficit of order $5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, not $0.74$. Either the cold bias sits where it does not
radiate to space, or several W\,m$^{-2}$ of compensating error are present. The comparison as it
stands cannot distinguish these, because \textbf{the TOA decomposition was measured on a PI run
against present-day CERES while the tropospheric bias was measured on the present-day run against
ERA5}. The clean version costs no model time: \texttt{amip\_presentday} already exists and
already reads net TOA $+2.20$ against CERES's $+0.97$. Running the band decomposition on
\emph{that} arm re-bases every energy target in the campaign onto a period-clean footing and
tests the OLR link directly.

\noindent \textbf{Two methodological lessons are worth as much as the physics.} A lever was once
promoted on a 4-year window at $+0.502$\,K and re-measured at $-0.038$ over 44 years --- pure

\section{The path forward}\label{sec:forward}
Ranked by expected value per unit of model time. Each carries its falsifier, stated in advance.

\textbf{1. The Southern Ocean cloud-area deficit (${\sim}6$\,pp).} The largest unexploited term
in the campaign: $+5.81$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ in-band, $+0.57$ global on its own, untouched by all 34
evaluated levers, and now with a \emph{larger} unexplained residual than before because aerosol
was measured out of it. The live mechanism is supercooled liquid: RH is $+5.8$ to $+6.6\,\%$ too
high through the mixed-phase layer while reflection is too low, and D2b --- the only lever that
has moved the pattern --- works by ice nucleation. Continue down that branch, and \textbf{score
on cloud area, not on CRE}, since amount and opacity are roughly half the error each and a CRE
metric cannot tell them apart.
\emph{Predicted:} closing the deficit buys ${\approx}0.5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of the imbalance at
near-zero global temperature cost.
\emph{Falsifier:} if the response also moves the tropics or NH T2m beyond noise, it is a global
cloud knob in disguise and must be rejected as A1a was.

\textbf{2. Re-base the energy target period-cleanly.} No model time at all: run the per-band TOA
decomposition on the existing \texttt{amip\_presentday} arm against CERES 2005--2015. That arm
reads $+2.20$ against CERES's $+0.97$, so the true radiative fault is ${\approx}+1.23$ --- twice
what the PI framing reports --- and every band target in this report currently carries an
unquantified epoch offset. It also supplies the direct test of the OLR/tropospheric-cold link of
\S\ref{sec:limiter}. \textbf{Do this before designing item 1's runs}, because it may move the
target.

\textbf{3. Diagnose the global tropospheric cold bias.} Now the dominant remaining term for land
2\,m temperature, the residual in the Siberian budget, the whole of the RIHMI growing-season
deficit, and the natural suspect for the residual imbalance. Fifty runs have not touched it, and
no lever built so far can. This needs \emph{diagnosis first} --- where the column loses its heat,
and whether the compensating error implied by the OLR arithmetic is real --- not another
parameter sweep.

\textbf{4. Verify K1 $+$ P5 $+$ G4 coupled.} Three things AMIP structurally cannot price. Whether
$48\,\%$ closure of the boreal bias suffices for forest survival, since the coupled model closes
a runaway that AMIP deliberately severs. Whether G4's delay of melt-out from 24 to 28 May
\emph{harms} forest establishment through later growing-season onset --- a real risk sitting
exactly where AMIP is blind. And the Arctic energy bill: prescribed SST and ice absorb
$1.7$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ without responding, and the coupled spin-up will not.

\textbf{5. The snow-free land albedo residual ($+0.0080$; crops $+0.0204$, semidesert $+0.0169$,
bare soil $+0.0145$).} The one remaining lever aimed at the ${\sim}0.7$\,K \emph{universal} bias
rather than the boreal one, untouched by all 50 runs. Worth ${\sim}0.12$\,K --- modest, but
cheap, and it is namelist-only. \emph{Caveat carried forward:} it must not simply be stacked on
the snow levers, because the tundra entry competes for the same Arctic energy budget.

\textbf{Explicitly not worth further runs:} \texttt{SWEMIN} optimisation (P3 and P5 differ by
$0.19$\,K in JJA, at or below noise, and the total available May cover error is worth
${\approx}+0.2$\,K); boreal-indexed surface levers beyond G4 (F5 $=$ F4, and the boreal-specific
budget is ${\sim}95\,\%$ spent); land albedo as an explanation for the universal bias (one sixth
of the target, measured).
