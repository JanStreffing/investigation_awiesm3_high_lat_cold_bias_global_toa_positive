\begin{abstract}\noindent
The short reading of a campaign whose full record runs to 128 pages
(\texttt{report.tex}). Three objectives throughout: the \textbf{global radiative
imbalance}, the \textbf{Southern Ocean}, and the \textbf{NH high-latitude cold bias /
boreal forest}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Adopt \texttt{RSBLB} 2.0.} Stable-boundary-layer mixing, exposed
as a namelist parameter this round. Coupled, it warms the Arctic winter screen by
$+0.67$\,K, all land by $+0.53$\,K in DJF, at \emph{zero} net TOA cost ($-0.027$) and
\emph{zero} Siberian summer cost. It gives \textbf{11R the best \texttt{cmpitool} score in
the campaign}, 0.7577 against the base's 0.8237, and it reverses the \texttt{tas}/\texttt{zg}
regression that 11G introduced. It is the first lever here to move winter and summer
targets in the same direction.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Retire S4 --- reversing this summary's predecessor.} The
2026--08--11 edition led with ``Adopt S4''. Measured properly, \texttt{RCL\_INPPMIN}
$70000\to50000$ buys nothing at the pole, authored the sub-Antarctic overcorrection that
blocks every remaining Southern Ocean lever, and its headline benefit \emph{reverses sign
coupled}: Siberian JJA $+1.194$\,K in AMIP against $-0.365$\,K coupled. Removal is under
test in 11V/11W; at 18 years it returns $+3.06$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ to the sub-Antarctic and
$-2.52$ to the polar band, both improvements, for $+0.27$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of energy.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The energy problem is a drift, not an offset.} The PI controls
start at net TOA $-0.06$ to $-0.08$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ and \emph{drift} to ${\sim}+1.0$ over 40
years, in every arm, 1850 and 1990 alike. A TOA drifting positive while the system warms
is backwards for a damped system; the cause is SH sea-ice loss of ${\sim}2\times10^6$\,km$^2$
over the same period. \textbf{The Southern Ocean does not respond to the imbalance, it
generates it.}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The remaining land error is above the screen.} At 60--90N in DJF
the air-side inversion bias is $+2.81$\,K while the skin-side bias is $+0.11$. Three
surface levers scored null because they were adjusting a part of the column carrying no
error. Of the $-2.23$\,K all-land DJF bias, ${\sim}1.0$\,K is excess inversion and
${\sim}1.2$\,K is a deep tropospheric cold bias no mixing lever can reach.
\end{abstract}

\section{The boreal forest: the founding problem, and where it stands}\label{sec:boreal}
The campaign began because the coupled model loses the Siberian boreal forest. That is now
partly understood and only partly fixed.

\textbf{It is not a climate gate.} BNS (larch) is permitted everywhere in Siberia and is
\emph{outcompeted by C3 grass} --- established 2026-07-14 and re-derived at least five
times since. Establishment thresholds, GDD5 and the climate gates are not what excludes
it, so no amount of warming the gate will restore it.

\textbf{Two mechanisms, both measured.} A summer route --- over-transpiration $\to$ moist
boundary layer $\to$ JJA cloud $+8.5$\,pp $\to$ $-7.0$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ surface SW --- and a
winter route through roughness: when LPJ-GUESS loses the forest, thermal roughness
collapses by three to four orders of magnitude (forest $z_{0h}=2.0$\,m against tundra
$3.4\times10^{-4}$), the surface decouples and the inversion deepens by $+2.1$\,K.

\textbf{What worked and what did not.} G4 (\texttt{RVRSMIN}) took ${\sim}0.95$ of the
boreal budget and saturates: F5, combining four land levers, gives $+0.746$ against F4
alone at $+0.749$. Raupach roughness cannot bootstrap the canopy it needs. The current
state is Siberian JJA $-1.53$\,K against ERA5 in 11Q, improved a full kelvin from the
base, with \texttt{RSBLB} contributing $+0.767$ of it --- the first winter lever that also
helped summer.

\section{Retire S4: the reversal}\label{sec:s4}
The predecessor to this document recommended adopting S4 on the strength of an AMIP
result. Three measurements since say otherwise.

\textbf{It buys nothing at the pole.} Ocean-masked, 30\,yr paired, 11G$-$11E: SW CRE at
90--60S DJF is $+0.77$ and \emph{not significant}, against $-2.34$\,* at 60--45S. A ratio
of $-0.33$: purely equatorward.

\textbf{It authored the blocking constraint.} That $-2.34$ is the largest single
contribution to the sub-Antarctic overcorrection, now $-3.70$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ DJF, which is
what disqualifies DMS, \texttt{RCL\_KK\_CLOUD\_NUM\_SEA} and every other SH-wide
brightening lever --- while the polar deficit stands at $+13.36$.

\section{The Southern Ocean has two problems, and it generates the imbalance}\label{sec:so}
\textbf{Every arm drifts, including the 1850 controls}: $+0.8$\,K of SH surface air and
$-2\times10^6$\,km$^2$ of September ice per 40 years, with the 1990 forcing adding only
$+0.2$\,K on top. At the branch point the SH T2m bias was $-0.06$ to $-0.65$\,K, i.e.\ the
air was \emph{not} warm --- so the ocean was not warmed by the atmosphere. Both warm
together, and net TOA drifts from ${\sim}-0.07$ to ${\sim}+1.0$ over the same 40 years.

Ocean-masked, the two Southern Ocean bands are the largest positive contributors to the
global imbalance, $+0.32$ and $+0.36$\,W\,m$^{-2}$, against a tropical $-1.09$. The global
figure is a small residual of large regional cancellations, so ``close the TOA imbalance
and the Southern Ocean follows'' is circular.

\textbf{Two mechanisms, seasonally separated.} In DJF the sea-ice zone absorbs
$+7.99$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ too much shortwave at the surface, from cloud that is present but
optically thin. In MAM the anomaly is $+4.48$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of \emph{longwave} with almost
no shortwave --- autumn mixed-layer deepening entrains a $+0.9$\,K warm anomaly sitting at
exactly 100\,m, which is why March ice never regrows (model $1.30$ against $3.10$
observed). The summer heat is stored and re-emerges in autumn; suppressing ocean mixing
makes the 100\,m layer \emph{worse}, so it is fed from above, not below.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{so_two_problems.png}
\caption{Two Southern Ocean problems in two different seasons. \textbf{(a)} The ice
seasonal cycle is too large: September is $+0.78\times10^6$\,km$^2$ in area against

\label{fig:sotwo}

\textbf{The cloud error is half amount and half opacity}, and the hemispheres need
\emph{opposite} corrections: Antarctic summer SW CRE is $+13.36$ (reflects too little)
while Arctic summer is $-18.22$ (reflects far too much). No lever tested is selective
enough to treat them separately at a useful dose.

At the pole, cover is $5.8$\,pp low \emph{and} each cloud is $6.7\,\%$ too dim, about
$6.5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ each of the $13.36$. In the sub-Antarctic there are $6.1$\,pp too few
clouds each $3\,\%$ too \emph{bright} --- they compensate, which is how the net CRE ended
up overcorrected. \textbf{Every campaign lever raises opacity}, which is why the two bands
diverged.

\textbf{Cloud phase flips sign at 60S.} Low-level liquid fraction against CALIPSO-GOCCP:
45--65S model $0.873$ vs $0.858$ (too liquid, $+0.015$); \textbf{60--90S $0.731$ vs
$0.759$} (too icy, $-0.028$); Siberia $0.813$ vs $0.818$. Icier cloud precipitates out and
carries less liquid water, which is how a cloud is present and optically thin. The INP
family was retired on a phase measurement taken in the one band where the model is too
liquid, and that conclusion does not extend poleward.

\textbf{Where the deficit sits.} Split by ice cover, DJF: the pack ($1.2\times10^6$\,km$^2$)
is nearly unbiased at $+1.47$; the deficit lives in the marginal ice zone
($10.0\times10^6$\,km$^2$, $+14.49$) and the open water south of 60S
($9.3\times10^6$\,km$^2$, $+15.86$). Any candidate must act on sunlit open water beside
ice, which rules out anything keyed to ice cover.

\textbf{The seasonal cycle is too large}: too much ice at the September maximum ($+0.78$
area, $+2.01$ extent) and far too little through February--May, with the March regrowth
absent entirely. The ice is \emph{not} too dark --- compact-ice September albedo is $0.760$

observed range. But \textbf{in January--March no cell anywhere exceeds 90\,\%
concentration}, so the area-mean albedo the sun sees falls to $0.49$--$0.51$. The pack is
too diffuse: mean concentration inside the ice edge is $0.767$ in September against
OSI-SAF's $0.804$, spread over $2\times10^6$\,km$^2$ more extent. Diffuse ice has less
volume per unit area to lose and melts out rather than retreating to a compact core.

\section{Forcing: what is pinned, and what is not}\label{sec:forcing}
\texttt{NCMIPFIXYR} pins GHG, ozone, composition, stratospheric aerosol, SO$_4$, solar TSI
\emph{and} MACv2SP. It does \textbf{not} pin LPJ-GUESS, which carries its own
\texttt{fixed\_CO2}, \texttt{fixed\_ndep} and \texttt{fixed\_LU}. Both must be set, or a
1990 arm runs present-day climate over pre-industrial CO$_2$ and land use.

Three traps, all hit:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item The \texttt{\&NAMECECMIP6} block \emph{accepts} \texttt{NCMIPFIXYR} and is
\textbf{never read}. Liveness must be checked as \texttt{Set JYEAR} in
\texttt{NODE.001\_01}. 50 AMIP arms ran transient because of it.
\item \texttt{SP\_SETUP: IYR} reports the MACv2SP \emph{file} that was opened, not the year
the profile is evaluated at --- that is \texttt{YEAR\_FR}, which honours
\texttt{NCMIPFIXYR}. A correct run was cancelled on this misreading and had to be
resubmitted unchanged.
\item \texttt{fixed\_LU} 1990 raises the peat-vs-LUH3 conflict count from 73 cells at 1850
to ${\sim}493$ at 2000; arms running the default \texttt{guess} silently over-allocate
those to peat.
\end{itemize}

\section{What the lever catalogue now says}\label{sec:levers}
A full kept/discarded table is in \texttt{tuning\_summary.pdf}. The load-bearing points:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{The stable-mixing trade is linear in dose.} Tropical SW CRE paid per K of
Arctic inversion removed is $0.88$, $0.87$, $3.43$ for \texttt{RSBLB} $b{=}3$, $b{=}2$ and
the mixing-length route. Constant across a factor-two dose change, so the cost cannot be
dodged by tuning gentler; \texttt{RSBLB} is the efficient frontier.
\item \textbf{Two verdicts were rendered against the wrong band and are reopened.} A1c
(\texttt{RDEPLIQREFDEPTH}) reads $-1.30$ on the polar ocean band and is nearly free.
\textbf{B2} (\texttt{RCLDIFF\_CONVI}) is the only arm in the catalogue that passes both
polar vetoes: polar $-0.68$\,*, Arctic $+0.13$ (null), sub-Antarctic $+0.18$ (null, the
helpful sign), and Siberia $+0.38$\,K as a bonus. It attacks the amount half that nothing
else reaches.
\item \textbf{DMS works at the pole but is not selective.} $-6.10$ at 90--60S DJF against
$-6.55$ at 60--45S, so it deepens the overcorrection that blocks it. Tropics $-2.35$,
global TOA $-1.61$. Shelved, not falsified.
\item \textbf{Ocean surface albedo is not the problem.} Open-water albedo tracks CERES to
within $0.007$ from 70S equatorward, including the elevated $0.101$ against $0.104$ at
70--60S where the sun is lowest. The zenith-angle dependence works.
\end{itemize}

\section{The energy budget: what closes, and what does not}\label{sec:energy}
\textbf{Snow enthalpy closes the column.} An apparent ``$-1.0$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ structural
leak, invariant across arms'' between TOA and surface was the omitted
$sf\cdot\rho\cdot L_f$ term: it measures $0.962$--$1.073$ against residuals of $-0.996$ to
$-1.088$. With it, the column conserves to $0.02$--$0.04$\,W\,m$^{-2}$. The convention is
$\text{surface}=ssr+str+sshf+slhf-sf\rho L_f$, and omitting it inverted a conclusion:
tuning net TOA to zero \emph{does} stop the drift.

\textbf{But the imbalance is not an offset.} The PI controls start at $-0.06$ to $-0.08$
and drift to ${\sim}+1.0$ over 40 years. Regionally, ocean-masked, the two Southern Ocean
bands contribute $+0.32$ and $+0.36$ against a tropical $-1.09$: the global number is a
residual of large cancellations, and tuning it as a scalar treats a symptom.

\textbf{Accumulated fluxes divide by 3600}, both for AMIP daily and coupled monthly means
--- verified empirically (global ASR $240.4$\,W\,m$^{-2}$), not assumed from the output
frequency.

\section{Traps that cost this campaign runs}\label{sec:traps}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Verify the staged artefact, not the source.} W1 ran 46 years identical to
its control because the staged library predated the namelist exposure. \texttt{surf}
parameters live in \texttt{libsurf.SP.so}, not \texttt{libarpifs.SP.so}.
\item \textbf{Two \texttt{lpj\_guess} binary generations exist.}
\texttt{production\_8c5ab467} for 11E/11G, \texttt{raupach\_junebase\_gated} for 11L--11R.
Comparisons spanning them are confounded --- including the 11E$\to$11N step in
\S\ref{sec:stands} and the \texttt{RSNOWLIN2} row of the meridional decomposition. All
arms are now pinned via \texttt{lpj\_guess.executable}. The test built to prove the code
change inert (\texttt{11H0\_codeonly}) never produced output.
\item \textbf{AMIP$\to$coupled transfer has been wrong in sign}, not just magnitude ---
S4's Siberian JJA. Transfer ratios across the campaign span $0.19$ to $1.01\times$.
\item \textbf{Band averages fold the Antarctic ice sheet into the polar ocean}, halving the
apparent signal: surface net SW bias reads $-0.57$ over the cap and $+7.99$ over ocean.
\item \textbf{Sea-ice AREA and EXTENT are different numbers} and mixing them inverts the
sign: September is $+0.78$ in area but $+2.01$ in extent.
\item \textbf{\texttt{clr\_t}, not \texttt{clr\_c}}, is the model-comparable CERES clear
sky. Worth $1.7$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ at the poles.
\item \texttt{NODE.001\_01} is ${\sim}2$\,GB with NUL bytes --- \texttt{grep -a} or it
silently reports nothing and looks like a failed gate.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open, in priority order}\label{sec:open}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{The drift.} $+1.0$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ and $-2\times10^6$\,km$^2$ of SH ice per 40
years, in every arm, addressed by no lever. It is the single largest obstacle to a
multi-millennial spin-up and it is what the S4 test is ultimately about.
\item \textbf{11V/11W}, deciding S4. Read the drift rate, not the snapshot.
\item \textbf{B2 at a stronger dose} on the current base --- the only untested candidate
with the right meridional shape, and the only one addressing cloud amount.
\item \textbf{The Arctic contradiction}: a $13$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ surface shortwave deficit
under a surface that is $+0.84$\,K \emph{warm}. Unexplained, and it is the campaign's
founding region.
\item \textbf{The deep ocean}, $-0.33$ to $-0.42$\,K at 500--1000\,m in every arm, genuinely
unaddressed. The \texttt{kpplow} family is the right tool but must follow the radiative fix,
not precede it.
\item \textbf{reval's printed \texttt{bias} annotations do not reproduce.}
For 11N it reads $-1.723$ against 11E's $-1.57$, reversing an ordering that a
consistent recomputation puts the other way ($-1.619$ against $-1.698$). Window,
reference or masking --- unresolved, and it affects how every reval panel should be read.
\item \textbf{reval's PHC3 ocean plots are empty for all five runs} --- byte-identical
153\,687-byte files. The ocean interior has never been validated by the tool.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The path forward}\label{sec:forward}
In order, with what each decides:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Finish 11V/11W} (running). Decides S4. Read the \emph{drift rate} over 40
years, not the snapshot: if removing S4 brightens the polar band by ${\sim}2.5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$
and the drift slows, the $+0.27$ energy penalty is repaid. If the drift is unchanged, the
penalty is just a penalty.
\item \textbf{B2 at a stronger dose} against SB0, AMIP. The only untested candidate with
the right meridional shape and the only one addressing cloud amount. Bracket it --- its
costs (tropics $+0.60$, TOA $+0.39$ at \texttt{RCLDIFF\_CONVI} $10\to25$) presumably scale.
\item \textbf{Adopt \texttt{RSBLB} into the spin-up configuration} unless the Arctic screen
trend continues to zero. Everything else in the stack is unchanged by it.
\item \textbf{Explain the Arctic contradiction} --- $13$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ of surface shortwave
deficit under a surface $+0.84$\,K warm. Analysis only; the runs exist.
\item \textbf{Then, and only then, the deep ocean.} $-0.33$ to $-0.42$\,K at
500--1000\,m in every arm. The \texttt{kpplow} family is the right tool, but applied before
the radiative fix it compensates for an error that is still present --- which is exactly
how the 06T/06V arms ended up too cold globally.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What is not worth another arm.} The mixed-phase opacity family (selectivity is
dose-dependent and dies at useful doses), DMS at any dose (not polar-selective), ocean
surface albedo (measured correct), and anything keyed to sea-ice cover (the deficit is
beside the ice, not on it).

\section{What would change the picture}\label{sec:falsifiers}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{\texttt{RSBLB} retired} if the Arctic DJF screen gain continues decaying at
$-0.46$\,K/decade and reaches zero, or if a longer coupled arm shows the sea-ice cost
resolving (SH September $-1.0\times10^6$\,km$^2$ in 11R is currently the one significant
ice change).
\item \textbf{S4 kept} if 11V/11W show Siberian JJA going backwards on removal.
\item \textbf{The whole cloud route closed} if B2 at a stronger dose loses its
selectivity the way \texttt{RDEPLIQREFRATE} did --- selectivity has been dose-dependent
every time it has been tested.
\item \textbf{The drift attributed elsewhere} if 11W shows the drift rate unchanged by a
$-2.5$\,W\,m$^{-2}$ polar brightening, which would mean SH ice-albedo is not what sustains
it.
\end{itemize}
