\part{Tuning away the high TOA imbalance}
\emph{We set out to cut the AWI--ESM3 PI-control's $+1.5$\,W/m$^2$ TOA imbalance. Every effective lever does it by
\textbf{cooling} the model, and the aggressive coupling/ocean-mixing levers cut the imbalance hardest --- but push the
planet too cold against ERA5. This part documents that trade-off across 22 runs and traces the aggressive lever to a
Southern-Ocean ocean-convection mechanism.}

\section{Goal \& setup}
Reduce the net top-of-atmosphere (TOA) radiative imbalance of the AWI--ESM3 pre-industrial control so it
equilibrates faster, \emph{without} degrading the CMIP6-normalised performance index (CMPI, \texttt{part4})
or inflating the 2\,m-temperature RMSE vs ERA5 (\texttt{part8}). 22 experiments, each a 30-year segment
(model years 1350--1379) of the CORE3/TCO95 configuration (FESOM2 CORE3 mesh, OpenIFS TCO95/A096, LPJ-GUESS),
all branched from one \textbf{Baseline}. The baseline PI-control still drifts with a positive TOA imbalance of
$\approx+1.5$\,W/m$^2$ (energy \emph{into} the system --- the signature of unequilibrated spin-up and/or ongoing
ocean heat uptake). \textbf{A caution on the target.} The tuning levers change the model's \emph{equilibrium}, not
its rate of approach, so ``lower imbalance'' is a useful \emph{ranking} objective but not automatically ``more
equilibrated'': a low-imbalance-but-cold state is not obviously better than a high-imbalance-but-unbiased one. This
is why the campaign's real aim (\S\ref{sec:rec}) is a \emph{warm-floor} state where the imbalance is cut by a
\emph{moderate} lever, not by cooling into a cold bias --- i.e.\ we treat imbalance and 2\,m-T bias as joint targets,
not imbalance alone.

\textbf{Structural note.} Almost every non-baseline run also carries a \emph{common melt-pond shift}
(\texttt{albpnd}\,$0.2\!\to\!0.28$, \texttt{rfracmax}\,$1.0\!\to\!0.75$, \texttt{pndaspect}\,$0.8\!\to\!1.3$).
Differences below are each run's \emph{distinguishing} change on top of that.

\textbf{Three parts.} \textbf{(I)} evaluates the 22 imbalance-tuning runs and exposes the imbalance$\leftrightarrow$cold-bias
trade-off, tracing the aggressive lever to a Southern-Ocean mechanism. \textbf{(II)} shows that the cold bias which
makes that lever costly is largely \emph{not} the tuning but a \textbf{structural} LPJ-GUESS boreal-forest collapse
(\S\ref{sec:lpjg}) --- a \emph{counter-lever}: fixing it warms the NH high latitudes, buying the RMSE headroom to spend
the aggressive global cooling on the imbalance. A decisive feedback-free experiment pins that fix to vegetation, not
climate. \textbf{(III)} combines them into a work plan: raise the structural warm floor, then cut the imbalance
(\S\ref{sec:rec}).

\section{Headline: the imbalance\,$\leftrightarrow$\,cold-bias trade-off}
Every lever that meaningfully lowers the TOA imbalance does so by \emph{cooling} the model, which worsens the
cold bias against ERA5. Two families separate cleanly: the sea-ice/albedo/melt-pond changes move gently
down-left (small imbalance gain, small bias cost); the coupling + ocean-mixing changes cut the imbalance hard
(30--57\,\%) but push the planet $1.7$--$2.1$\,K too cold.

\textbf{The cold-bias axis is two things at once.} It mixes (i) a \emph{shared structural floor} --- every run,
including the baseline and the ``clean'' melt-pond runs, carries a strong Siberia/N.\ Canada cold bias that is
\emph{not} a tuning artefact but an LPJ-GUESS boreal-forest-cover bug (\S\ref{sec:lpjg}); and (ii) the \emph{differential},
tuning-induced global cooling that the aggressive runs add on top. The structural floor is the same for all
points and is independently fixable; the tuning only moves runs vertically relative to it. This is why the
``warming lever'' in \S\ref{sec:rec} is the LPJG fix, not another atmospheric retune.

\subsection{Radiation budget (\texttt{part2})}\label{sec:rad}
Surface net flux tracks TOA to within $\approx0.1$\,W/m$^2$ in every run --- i.e.\ the atmospheric column stores
essentially no energy on these multi-year means, as expected. Note this does \emph{not} by itself localise the
\emph{cause}: a TOA\,$\approx$\,surface match is equally consistent with a genuine TOA radiative error and with
ocean heat uptake / unequilibrated spin-up drift passing cleanly through the surface (\S\ref{sec:so},
\S\ref{sub:withwithout}). What it rules out is an atmosphere-only bookkeeping leak, so the imbalance and the surface
energy budget can be read interchangeably. Baseline $\approx+1.53$\,W/m$^2$; the coupling+mixing stack drives it
down to $+0.66$\,W/m$^2$ (06V).

\subsection{CMPI (\texttt{part4})}\label{sec:cmpi}
CMPI integrates 13 fields (siconc, tas, clt, pr, rlut, uas, vas, ua@300, zg@500, zos, mlotst, thetao, so) over
regions/seasons, normalised against 30 CMIP6 models; \textbf{$<1$ = better than the CMIP6 ensemble-mean error}.
Run on a subset spanning the trade-off front (now complete for all 22 runs).

\textbf{How to read this (important).} CMPI here compares a \emph{PI-control} run to \emph{present-day}
observations, so a moderate global cold offset --- and hence some CMPI rise --- is \emph{expected} and is not
by itself a disqualifier. The informative question is not ``did CMPI go up?'' but ``\emph{where} did the error
change, and is that consistent with the parameter that was altered?'' A sea-ice-albedo change should move
\texttt{siconc} in the polar regions and little else; if it instead degrades the tropics, that is a red flag.
The per-variable / per-region breakdown below is the tool for that judgement; the global number is a summary,
not a verdict.

\section{Southern-Ocean mechanism (adapted from a270234's notebooks)}\label{sec:so}
To see \emph{why} the ocean-mixing/coupling levers act as they do, two of a270234's Weddell-Sea diagnostics
(\texttt{mld\_sic\_sit\_compare}, \texttt{temp\_fesom\_core3\_WWS}) were re-pointed at the coupled
\texttt{Tuning\_test\_06} runs (same CORE3 mesh). Panels show the Baseline absolute field plus each run
\emph{minus} Baseline, austral winter (September), yr1370--79. (The \texttt{temp\_fesom\_core3\_WWS} tripyview
transect could not decode these runs' pre-1582 \texttt{gregorian} calendar, so an equivalent pyfesom2
depth--latitude section was used.)

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{plots/mld_winter_antarctic.png}
\caption{Antarctic September mixed-layer depth (MLD3): Baseline \emph{absolute} (top-left, own scale), others $=$
\emph{anomaly} vs Baseline on a symmetric $\pm70$\,m diverging scale (98th-pctile auto-scale; the intense
Weddell/Ross convection core saturates it). The mixing runs (06N/O/T/V) restructure the Weddell/Ross winter mixing
relative to Baseline, whereas melt-pond (06A) barely changes it. Deep open-ocean winter mixing there is FESOM
``open-ocean convection'' that ventilates warm deep water --- a known driver of the Southern-Ocean warm bias
(Heuz\'e et al.\ 2013; Heuz\'e 2021) and of TOA imbalance.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{plots/temp_wws_section_tuning.png}
\caption{West Weddell Sea (50--30$^{\circ}$W) September potential-temperature section: Baseline absolute and
anomalies. The ventilated mid-column cools in the mixing runs (less convective entrainment of warm deep water to the
surface), consistent with suppressed open-ocean convection; note that suppressing convection should leave the
\emph{deepest} water trapped/warmer rather than colder, and a near-surface edge warm anomaly is also present ---
i.e.\ the signal is a redistribution between layers, not uniform subsurface cooling.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{plots/sit_winter_antarctic.png}
\caption{Antarctic September sea-ice \emph{thickness} (Baseline absolute, top-left; others $=$ anomaly vs Baseline).
Thickness --- not concentration --- is the honest growth diagnostic here: winter concentration is already near
$1$ over most of the pack, so it \emph{saturates} and cannot register added ice, whereas thickness is unbounded. The
mixing runs (06O/06T/06V) thicken the pack broadly (positive/red anomaly over the Weddell and Ross sectors),
consistent with suppressed open-ocean convection insulating and stratifying the surface; melt-pond (06A) barely
changes it. (The concentration version, \texttt{sic\_winter\_antarctic.png}, shows only the ice-edge shifts for the
reason above.)}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Mechanism.} The mospp / weaker-KPP changes reduce Weddell-Sea open-ocean winter convection, cooling the
subsurface and letting sea ice grow --- improving \texttt{thetao}/\texttt{so}/\texttt{siconc} (consistent with the
CMPI breakdown) and removing the Southern-Ocean surface warm bias. Spurious open-ocean deep convection in the
Weddell/Ross gyres is the canonical CMIP Antarctic bias (Heuz\'e et al.\ 2013; Heuz\'e 2021, which evaluates AWI--CM
in the multi-model set; Mohrmann et al.\ 2021 for the polynya surface expression), and open-ocean Weddell convection
is a real-but-rare mode that models over-express (de Lavergne et al.\ 2014) --- so damping it is physically the right
direction. The \emph{proximate radiative} closure is defensible: more winter ice $\to$ higher albedo $\to$ less
absorbed SW $\to$ lower imbalance. We are more cautious about the ``reduced ocean heat uptake'' half of the chain:
suppressing convection also \emph{traps} heat at depth, which can \emph{increase} deep ocean-heat-content drift, so
the net uptake change is not established here --- it should be pinned to an OHC-tendency diagnostic before being
asserted. The cost is that the surface cooling propagates globally into the atmosphere (\texttt{tas}/\texttt{zg}),
the broad cold bias seen in \texttt{part8}.

\textbf{Counter-literature (a compensating-error risk this campaign must own).} \S\ref{sec:so} attributes the SO warm
bias and its removal almost entirely to \emph{ocean} convection, but Hyder et al.\ (2018, \emph{Nat.\ Commun.})
trace the leading $40$--$60$\textdegree S warm-SST bias primarily to \emph{atmospheric} net surface-heat-flux errors
dominated by shortwave/cloud biases, and warn that a small \emph{net} flux bias is often achieved by
\emph{compensating} component errors. The two coexist at different scales --- broad atmospheric-SW warmth vs.\
localised Weddell/Ross deep-convection anomalies --- and our mixing levers act only on the ocean branch. If part of
the bias is atmospheric-SW in origin, the ocean-mixing lever is partly compensating for an atmospheric error, which
is \emph{exactly} the ``cooling leaks beyond where the parameter should act'' failure mode flagged for these runs in
\S\ref{sec:cmpi} (the region map reddens into the tropics/Ni\~no-3.4). This tension is the report's own evidence
pointing at Hyder; it strengthens, not weakens, the case for treating aggressive ocean-mixing tunings as
provisional.

\part{Counter-tuning the NH cold bias --- the structural floor}
\emph{The cold bias that makes Part~I's aggressive lever expensive is largely \textbf{not} the tuning: it is a
structural LPJ-GUESS boreal-forest collapse that every run inherits. That makes it a \textbf{counter-lever} --- fixing
it warms the NH high latitudes at essentially unchanged imbalance, buying the RMSE headroom to spend the aggressive
global cooling on the imbalance. This part attributes the bias with a clean WITH-vs-WITHOUT-LPJG experiment, resolves
its two mechanisms and a residual, and --- with a decisive feedback-free spin-up --- pins the fix to vegetation
competition, not climate.}

\section{The NH high-latitude cold bias is a structural LPJ-GUESS problem}\label{sec:lpjg}
The runs carry a severe near-surface \textbf{cold bias over Siberia and northern Canada} (visible in every
\texttt{part8} map). This chapter documents a working investigation into whether the coupled dynamic vegetation
(LPJ-GUESS) is responsible --- motivated by the idea that fixing it could be a \emph{warming} lever to offset the
imbalance-tuning cold bias, and by prior EC-Earth experience that LPJG drives such boreal biases.

\subsection{Symptom: boreal needleleaf replaced by grass}
Dominant-PFT maps (tool \texttt{part26}) for the HTESSEL spin-up (the reference, run with the same atmosphere but
\emph{without} atmosphere$\leftrightarrow$LPJG feedback) vs the coupled runs show boreal needleleaf giving way to
C3 grass over Siberia / N.\ Canada. Tree-dominant fraction north of 50\textdegree N: \textbf{20.1\,\% (spin-up)
$\to$ 16.8\,\% (baseline) $\to$ 11.4\,\% (06V)} --- progressively worse with the more aggressive (colder) runs.
\emph{Caveat on the prior:} DGVMs disagree substantially on boreal tree-vs-grass partitioning, and real boreal
forest is genuinely open with substantial non-tree ground cover (Jiang et al.\ 2012), so ``grass over Siberia'' is
not \emph{automatically} a bug. What makes the establishment-failure reading defensible here is the \emph{magnitude}:
the coupled state reaches cvh\,$\approx$\,0.02--0.05 with \dead{no closed canopy anywhere (FORESTFPC$=0$)}\footnote{\textbf{Void diagnostic.} \texttt{FORESTFPC} sums only PFTs with \texttt{landcover==FOREST} (\texttt{commonoutput.cpp}:1143), i.e.\ the \emph{managed-forest} land-use class; natural trees are counted in \texttt{TREEFPC} via the \texttt{NATURAL} branch. In this PI configuration nothing is allocated to FOREST (\texttt{Natural\_sum}$=0.684$, \texttt{Total}$=0.665$), so \texttt{FORESTFPC}$\equiv0$ \emph{by construction} while \texttt{TREEFPC}$=0.323$. It is a land-use bookkeeping artifact and says nothing about canopy closure.} (
\S\ref{sec:workplan}), an outlier well below the $\sim0.4$--$0.7$ closed-taiga range (MODIS VCF), not merely a
grass-leaning partition.

\subsection{Is it grass growing, or trees dying? And is it a year-1 jump or a slow drift?}
Belt-mean (55--75\textdegree N) needleleaf LAI is essentially \emph{conserved} over the baseline run (0.40$\to$0.37,
trend $\approx$0), while C3 grass LAI \emph{rises} (0.65$\to$0.83, $+$0.10/dec): the signal is \textbf{grass
growing}, with needleleaf loss only regional (E.\ Siberia) and offset by gains in the milder west. The coupled
run is initialised directly from the spin-up's year-3850 LPJG state, and baseline year-1350 boreal LAI
(tree 0.407, grass 0.649) is \emph{identical} to the spin-up end-state (0.401 / 0.669) --- so there is
\textbf{no wholesale year-1 collapse}; the degraded, grass-heavy state is \emph{inherited from the spin-up}.
Within 1350--1379 the boreal 2\,m temperature is at its (cold) level from year one and does \emph{not} drift
down (trend $+$0.42\,K/dec, noisy) --- consistent with a memory-less atmosphere that equilibrates to the surface
state within a year rather than a slow vegetation runaway. (These are belt-mean \emph{LAI} diagnostics; as
\S\ref{sub:withwithout} shows, the quantity that actually matters is not LAI-per-tile but the forest \emph{cover fraction}.)

\subsection{The decisive test: a clean WITH-vs-WITHOUT-LPJG pair (CORE3, 30\,yr)}\label{sub:withwithout}
The earlier control was confounded by the ocean (core2 vs core3). We removed that confound: a purpose-built
\textbf{no-LPJG AWI-CM3-develop reference} --- \emph{same} CORE3 ocean, \emph{same} TCO95 atmosphere and matched
component branches, HTESSEL\,+\,prescribed satellite vegetation, a full 30-yr PI-control (model years 1350--1379,
\texttt{awicm3\_noLPJG\_CORE3\_30y}) --- differs from the LPJG Baseline in \textbf{LPJG alone}, so the 2\,m-T
difference is now a clean attribution. Its net TOA imbalance is $+1.73$\,W/m$^2$ (\texttt{part2}), near the
warm/high-imbalance end of Fig.~1 --- confirming the LPJG signal is a near-surface \emph{land} effect, orthogonal
to the TOA tuning of \S2. \textbf{Crucially, it beats the LPJG Baseline on \emph{both} skill metrics: global 2\,m-T RMSD vs ERA5 1.514\,K
(bias $-0.76$\,K, \texttt{part8}) vs 1.717/$-0.94$, and CMPI 0.738 (\texttt{part4}) vs 0.745} --- at higher
(warmer) imbalance. Removing LPJG improves the global temperature fit \emph{and} the multivariate skill score
\emph{while} the imbalance changes by only $+0.2$\,W/m$^2$ ($+1.73$ vs $+1.53$) --- within the inter-run drift of
these unequilibrated 30-yr segments (\S\ref{sec:caveats}), so the warming lever is real \emph{and costs essentially
no imbalance to within that uncertainty} (not, strictly, ``zero''). Over the final decade, boreal DJF:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Forest cover collapses.} Siberian high-veg cover \texttt{cvh}: \textbf{0.63 (no LPJG) $\to$ 0.02
(LPJG)} (pan-boreal 0.49\,$\to$\,0.07). LPJG passes essentially no forest to the atmosphere.
\item \textbf{And it is genuinely colder --- confound gone.} With the ocean held fixed, the LPJG run is
unambiguously \textbf{$-3.1$\,K} colder over Siberia in DJF ($-30.4$ vs $-27.4$\textdegree C).
\item \textbf{Snow-masking (spring albedo)} operates as before: forest removal brightens the snow season
(MAM \textbf{+0.077}). This is the canonical boreal-forest climate mechanism --- a masked snowpack lowers albedo and
warms high latitudes, and its removal cools most in spring (Bonan, Pollard \& Thompson 1992; Betts 2000; Bonan 2008),
with tree/canopy cover setting the snow-albedo-feedback magnitude that many models misrepresent (Loranty et al.\
2014; Chapin et al.\ 2005) --- so a model that loses its forest gets exactly this feedback wrong. But it is a
\emph{sunlight} effect and cannot explain the equally-cold polar night (\S\ref{sub:seasonality}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Seasonality: which months carry the cold effect}\label{sub:seasonality}
Resolving the same clean CORE3 WITH$-$WITHOUT pair by \emph{month} (boreal box 55--70\textdegree N, Siberia + N.\ America,
final decade) shows the LPJG cold effect is \textbf{present in every month except high summer}. The WITH$-$WITHOUT
2\,m-T difference is negative from September through June and only vanishes in July--August
($-0.1$/$-0.2$\,K), peaking in the \emph{shoulder/dark} seasons: \textbf{November $-3.9$\,K, April $-3.3$\,K},
with DJF $-2.4$\,K and MAM $-2.5$\,K (annual box-mean $-2.0$\,K). \textbf{This is the key seasonal fingerprint}: a
pure snow-albedo (sunlit) mechanism would confine the cold to the bright spring, yet the effect is
\emph{as large in near-sunless November/December} --- which is exactly what motivates the winter
roughness$\to$inversion mechanism of \S\ref{sub:roughness}. The near-zero July--August signal also confirms the
growing-season atmosphere is unaffected (consistent with JJA gates being cleared, \S\ref{sub:withwithout}).
Overlaid is the independent \textbf{OIFS--AMIP} run (\S\ref{sub:amipgen}: observed-SST, no-LPJG, PI 1870--79) --- it
tracks the \emph{warm} WITHOUT curve (annual $-7.6$ vs $-6.3$\,\textdegree C; DJF $-25.6$ vs $-24.9$; MAM even milder),
confirming that a feedback-free, realistically-forced boreal climate is warm, and that the forcing we drive the
decisive offline re-spin with (\S\ref{sub:amipgen}) is \emph{not} itself cold-biased.

\subsection{A second, winter mechanism: roughness $\to$ inversion}\label{sub:roughness}
Snow-albedo needs sunlight, yet the cold bias is as strong in DJF as in MAM. The missing forest acts a second way,
in the dark. OpenIFS roughness length is cover-weighted by \texttt{cvh}, and needleleaf forest ($z_{0m}=2.0$\,m) is
$20$--$60\times$ rougher than the tundra/grass ($0.034$--$0.10$\,m, \texttt{susveg\_mod.F90}) that replaces it. A
smoother surface couples less efficiently to the atmosphere; in polar night the stable boundary layer (SBL) decouples
and the surface radiatively cools, deepening the near-surface inversion. That surface--atmosphere coupling strength in
the SBL controls screen temperature and inversion depth is well established (Holtslag et al.\ 2013; Sandu et al.\
2013); we assemble the specific vegetation-roughness\,$\to$\,SBL-decoupling\,$\to$\,screen-T chain from the
land-surface and SBL literatures rather than a single published result, so it is presented as a mechanism the data
then test, not a settled citation. \textbf{The data show exactly this fingerprint}
(DJF Siberia, LPJG\,$-$\,no-LPJG): a \textbf{+2.1\,K stronger} low-level inversion (T$_{925}-$T$_{2m}$), a
\textbf{+0.7\,K stronger} surface inversion (T$_{2m}-$T$_{skt}$), \textbf{unchanged cloud} ($\Delta\approx0.00$) and
\textbf{$-4.7$\,W/m$^2$ less} down-welling longwave --- a decoupled, radiatively-cooling stable surface, not a
cloud/radiation artefact. Spatially over boreal land the forest-loss pattern \emph{is} the inversion pattern:
area-weighted $r(\Delta\texttt{cvh},\Delta\text{inv})=-0.60$, $r(\Delta\texttt{cvh},\Delta\text{sfc-decouple})=-0.68$,
$r(\Delta T_{2m},\Delta\text{inv})=-0.83$, while the negative control $r(\Delta\texttt{cvh},\Delta\text{cloud})=-0.05$
is $\approx0$ --- a real mechanism, not a shared trend. Regression lever: \textbf{restoring \texttt{cvh} by $+0.4$
warms DJF screen-T by $\sim+1.2$\,K} through roughness alone and weakens the inversion by $\sim0.7$\,K --- additive
to the spring/albedo warming. This slope-based estimate inherits the scatter of the underlying correlation
($r=0.63$, so $<40\%$ of grid-cell variance explained); read it as $\sim+1.2\pm0.5$\,K, an order-of-magnitude lever
rather than a precise prediction. The \emph{direction} and existence of the effect rest on the WITH$-$WITHOUT
experimental design (\S\ref{sub:withwithout}), not on this regression.

\subsection{How much does the LPJG fix buy? (vs ERA5)}\label{sub:levervsera5}
Against ERA5 the current Baseline is \textbf{$-4.5$\,K} too cold over Siberia in DJF. The lever helps but does not
close it: a realistic partial fix ($+1$\,K) leaves $-3.5$\,K, and \emph{full} forest restoration (the no-LPJG run
itself --- the ceiling) reaches \textbf{$-1.4$\,K}. So vegetation/roughness accounts for $\sim$3\,K, roughly
two-thirds of the Siberian DJF cold bias, and boreal-land RMSD falls 4.4\,$\to$\,3.3\,K --- but a residual $\sim$1.5\,K
survives \emph{even with perfect forest}.

\subsection{The residual driver: an over-strong stable-boundary-layer inversion}\label{sub:residual}
Diagnosing that residual in the \emph{full-forest} run (vegetation thereby ruled out) against ERA5 (DJFM, boreal
land) points to a single process. The model's low-level inversion is \textbf{+1.4\,K too strong}
(T$_{925}-$T$_{2m}$: model $+5.6$ vs ERA5 $+4.2$\,K), and the excess is \textbf{entirely surface-trapped} --- the
$925$--$1000$\,hPa inversion bias is $+0.07$\,K ($\approx0$), so all the extra cooling sits in the lowest layer.
Spatially this inversion-excess explains \textbf{$r=+0.76$} of \emph{where} the model is too cold; the Siberian
High (MSLP bias $+0.15$\,hPa, $r=0.27$) and cloud-\emph{fraction} (bias $+0.07$, $r=0.00$) are ruled out. A
too-strong wintertime polar inversion is a real, observationally-benchmarked model bias (Medeiros et al.\ 2011;
Zhang et al.\ 2011), which supports reading the ``$+1.4$\,K too strong'' residual as genuine. That surface-trapped
signature, with circulation and cloud-fraction eliminated, is also the textbook fingerprint of a \textbf{stable
boundary layer that under-mixes} --- the IFS-family ``too-stable'' winter cold bias (Sandu et al.\ 2013). Its lever
is the OpenIFS stable-BL turbulent mixing --- the ``long-tail'' stability functions / minimum stable diffusion whose
lineage runs Louis (1979) $\to$ Beljaars \& Viterbo (1998) --- \emph{orthogonal} to the vegetation fix.

\textbf{Two counters this diagnosis must own} (both align with the report's own ``compensating error'' worry).
\emph{(a) The mixing fix is a documented compensating error:} enhanced long-tail stable mixing is stronger than
LES/observations justify and is retained only because reducing it degrades the large-scale flow --- it improves 2\,m
temperature but deepens cyclones and harms low cloud (Sandu et al.\ 2013; Holtslag et al.\ 2013), so Phase~1B must
check the storm-track/low-cloud climatology (which CMPI/\texttt{part8} will not catch cleanly). \emph{(b) The inversion
may be a cloud-phase problem:} Pithan et al.\ (2014) attribute much of the modelled Arctic wintertime cold bias to
missing mixed-phase low cloud (a LW, cloud-\emph{phase} effect) --- our null control ruled out cloud \emph{fraction}
but not phase, so we scope the residual claim to ``given cloud fraction is unbiased'' and flag a cloud-phase /
LW$\downarrow$ test as a prerequisite. Likewise the $r=+0.76$ residual-to-inversion match is suggestive, not proof
that the ``fourth lever'' is truly orthogonal.

\textbf{Near-complete attribution.} The two mechanisms close the boreal DJF cold bias between them: of the
$\approx-4.5$\,K Siberian bias vs ERA5, the LPJG forest-cover collapse (snow-albedo in spring, roughness in winter)
contributes $\approx-3$\,K, and the over-strong stable-BL inversion the remaining $\approx-1.4$\,K. Two
\emph{orthogonal} levers --- the LPJG establishment fix and enhanced stable-BL mixing --- therefore target
essentially the entire bias. (The veg cooling is also a \emph{stable} offset, not a runaway: over 20\,yr the LPJG
run carries more/thicker Arctic ice as a bounded mean offset with no amplifying sea-ice drift.)

\subsection{Working conclusion \& lever}
LPJ-GUESS sends essentially no boreal \emph{forest cover} to OpenIFS (cvh $\sim0.6\to0.02$ over Siberia,
\S\ref{sub:withwithout}), so the canopy no longer masks snow (spring albedo, \S\ref{sub:withwithout}) \emph{nor}
roughens the surface (winter inversion, \S\ref{sub:roughness}) --- a physically robust driver of the
Siberia/N.\ Canada cold bias, consistent with the EC-Earth family in which LPJ-GUESS is interactively coupled
(D\"oscher et al.\ 2022) and in which dynamic vegetation-cover / effective-LAI improves high-latitude spring climate
through the snow-albedo coupling (Alessandri et al.\ 2017). Source trace (verified): LPJG computes the high-veg fraction and
exports it over OASIS as \texttt{GUE\_FRAH}$\to$\texttt{cvh} (\texttt{framework.cpp::computeDailyLAIandFPCforIFS};
\texttt{namcouple} line 120). With the AWI setting \texttt{iftreefracca=1} (\texttt{global.ins}:151), cvh is the
summed tree \emph{crown area}$\times$density (no 0.05 clamp --- that constant is dead code; \texttt{natural\_frac}$=1$
over PI land), so cvh$\approx$0.05 means boreal tree cover is genuinely $\sim$5\%. The establishment gates
(\texttt{gdd5min\_est=500}, \texttt{twmin\_est=5}) are \emph{not} the blocker --- they are cleared across the boreal ---
so this is a \textbf{tree-vs-grass competition failure} (grass out-produces trees at canopy closure; settled in
\S\ref{sub:decisive}), with the degraded state \emph{inherited from the LPJG spin-up}, not a coupled runaway. Seasonally (\S\ref{sub:seasonality})
the LPJG cold effect spans September--June (DJF $-2.4$, MAM $-2.5$\,K over the boreal box)
and vanishes only in July--August --- i.e.\ it is a cold-season, not a growing-season, effect. The co-equal
\emph{sunless} late-autumn/winter part --- which pure snow-albedo cannot explain --- is now
resolved (\S\ref{sub:withwithout}--\ref{sub:residual}): it is the roughness$\to$inversion effect of the lost
forest (\S\ref{sub:roughness}) plus a model-intrinsic over-strong stable-BL inversion (\S\ref{sub:residual}).

\textbf{Fixes}: (1) tune the boreal \textbf{tree-vs-grass competition} (\texttt{greff\_min}, C3-grass competitiveness,
cold-tree photosynthesis \texttt{pstemp\_low}) --- \emph{not} the establishment gates, which are correct
(\S\ref{sub:decisive}) --- and re-do the LPJG spin-up; highest value, cheap to screen offline by checking cvh recovers
toward the MODIS pattern; (2) revisit \texttt{iftreefracca}/crown allometry; (3) a woody stem-area floor on the
snow-masking fraction in OpenIFS
(\texttt{surfbc\_ctl\_mod.F90}:389--391 / \texttt{surfrad\_ctl\_mod.F90}:650--655) --- but this only bites once (1)
raises cvh, since the masked-snow tile is weighted by cvh itself. Fixes (1)--(3) are the physically-grounded
\emph{warming lever} of \S\ref{sec:rec} and buy $\sim$3\,K (\S\ref{sub:levervsera5}). \textbf{A fourth, orthogonal
lever} attacks the residual $\sim$1.4\,K (\S\ref{sub:residual}): \emph{enhanced stable-boundary-layer mixing} in
OpenIFS (VDF stability functions / minimum stable diffusion), which would relax the over-strong surface inversion.
Together (1)--(4) target essentially the entire boreal DJF cold bias.

\subsection{Decisive test: competition, not climate \textnormal{---
\textcolor{red}{substantially qualified, see \S\ref{sub:xfer}}}}\label{sub:decisive}
\fbox{\parbox{0.97\textwidth}{\small\textbf{Read this section with \S\ref{sub:xfer} alongside.} The argument
below turns on a standalone LPJG re-spin driven by the OIFS--AMIP forcing under-vegetating despite the
establishment gates being cleared, which was read as an LPJG-internal competition failure. A later controlled
experiment (\S\ref{sub:xfer}) shows that \emph{moving between the CRUNCEP and AMIP forcings alone}, with
byte-identical parameters, costs $-54\,\%$ of Siberian and $-66\,\%$ of East-Siberian \texttt{TREEFPC}. The
under-vegetation seen here is therefore largely what the forcing change predicts on its own, and cannot by
itself demonstrate a competition failure. \dead{What does survive is FORESTFPC$=0$ --- no closed canopy forms
under either forcing --- so the closed-canopy failure remains unexplained and may still be competition.}
\texttt{FORESTFPC} is in fact a \emph{void diagnostic} here: it counts only the managed-forest land-use class,
which is empty in this PI configuration, so it is identically zero whatever the vegetation does
(\S\ref{sub:xfer}). There is no separate closed-canopy failure.
\dead{Verdict: the deficit is primarily an LPJG-internal competition problem, not a climate one.}}}
Is the boreal forest deficit a \emph{climate} problem (fix the atmosphere) or an \emph{LPJG-internal} tree-vs-grass
competition problem (fix the vegetation)? We answered it directly with a \textbf{feedback-free} experiment: a clean
OpenIFS--AMIP run (prescribed observed SST, no coupled ocean/LPJG cold feedback; $\sim$PI 1870--79) generated a
realistic daily forcing, which drove a 2000-yr standalone LPJG re-spin on a \emph{dense} 11\,538-cell gridlist
(build detail in \S\ref{sub:amipgen}). Fig.~\ref{fig:decisive} puts that forcing (top) beside the equilibrium
vegetation it produced (bottom). \textbf{The forcing is \emph{not} growing-season-cold}: over the Siberian box the
warmest-month T2m is $14.7$\,\textdegree C (100\% of cells clear the \texttt{twmin\_est}$=+5$ gate) and GDD5 is $730$
(76\% clear $500$) --- establishment is climatically permitted essentially everywhere trees should be. \textbf{Yet the
spin-up under-vegetates and grass wins}: cvh$=0.21$ (far below the $0.4$--$0.7$ closed-taiga range),
\texttt{GRASSFPC}$=0.37 > \texttt{TREEFPC}=0.21$, and \dead{no closed canopy forms anywhere (FORESTFPC$=0$)}\footnote{\textbf{Void diagnostic.} \texttt{FORESTFPC} sums only PFTs with \texttt{landcover==FOREST} (\texttt{commonoutput.cpp}:1143), i.e.\ the \emph{managed-forest} land-use class; natural trees are counted in \texttt{TREEFPC} via the \texttt{NATURAL} branch. In this PI configuration nothing is allocated to FOREST (\texttt{Natural\_sum}$=0.684$, \texttt{Total}$=0.665$), so \texttt{FORESTFPC}$\equiv0$ \emph{by construction} while \texttt{TREEFPC}$=0.323$. It is a land-use bookkeeping artifact and says nothing about canopy closure.}, with grass
filling exactly the East-Siberian interior. On a dense gridlist this cannot be dismissed as under-sampling.
\textbf{Verdict: the deficit is primarily an LPJG-internal competition problem, not a climate one} --- so the primary
counter-lever is the vegetation model (boreal \texttt{greff\_min}, C3-grass competitiveness, \texttt{pstemp\_low}), not
another atmospheric retune. The one climate co-limit is the deep-winter cold ($-28.3$\,\textdegree C box mean,
$<-40$ in NE Siberia) that gates out evergreens on $\sim40\%$ of cells --- but climatically-permitted \emph{larch}
(no cold gate) fails there too (BNS cover $0.035$), so the winter warming of Phase~1B is only a \emph{secondary} aid.

\part{The path forward: raise the floor, then tune}
\emph{Parts~I--II give the strategy: the imbalance can only be cut by cooling, and cooling is only affordable once the
structural NH cold floor is raised. So first fix the floor (vegetation competition + stable-BL mixing), \textbf{then}
spend a moderate cooling lever to reach surface balance --- now without the RMSE blow-up. First the caveats that
condition this, then the recommendation and a phased plan.}

\section{Caveats}\label{sec:caveats}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{06D ``HRlike'' has no HR-specific namelist change} --- only the common pond shift (identical to 06C);
its metrics confirm this. If a resolution/timestep/viscosity change was intended, it is not in the namelists.
\item \textbf{Filename convention.} These runs write \texttt{atm\_remapped\_1m\_<var>\_YYYY-YYYY.nc} (no
\texttt{\_1m\_} infix). I added backward-compatible fallbacks to \texttt{part2}, \texttt{part8} and the CMPI
preprocessing; existing configs are unaffected.
\item \textbf{Accumulation period} for these XIOS runs is \textbf{3600\,s} (not the 21600\,s of the old HR config);
configs use \texttt{accumulation\_period=3600} (verified: tsr\,$\to\approx240$\,W/m$^2$).
\item None of the runs is at equilibrium within 30\,yr --- numbers are a ranking, not final values.

\section{Recommendation \& synthesis}\label{sec:rec}
Reading global CMPI/RMSD as a guide rather than a gate (the PI-vs-PD offset is expected), the picture is:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item The \textbf{melt-pond / albedo family (06A--06D, etc.)} are \emph{clean}: they improve sea ice where they
should and leave everything else essentially untouched. Their imbalance gain is modest ($5$--$20\%$) but it comes
with no unexpected collateral --- safe building blocks.
\item The \textbf{ocean-mixing / coupling family (06O/T)} delivers the only large imbalance reductions
($30$--$38\%$) \emph{and} genuinely improves the ocean interior and sea ice --- but the cooling leaks into the
atmosphere globally (incl.\ tropics), which is degradation in places the parameter should not reach. That makes
them promising but not yet ``free.''
\item \textbf{06V} pushes too far: the \texttt{ENTSTPC3} convection change degrades \texttt{tas}/\texttt{zg}
everywhere --- not recommended as-is.
\end{itemize}
Best-balanced single steps: \textbf{06P} ($-29\%$, well-localised) and \textbf{06H} ($-20\%$).

\textbf{Synthesis --- and where the warming lever comes from.} The two arcs join here. The imbalance can only be
cut substantially by cooling levers (ocean mixing/coupling), so every combo tried so far overshoots into cold
bias. But \S\ref{sec:lpjg} shows the shared Siberia/N.\ Canada cold lobe is \emph{not} a tuning artefact, and a
purpose-built clean no-LPJG CORE3 reference (\S\ref{sub:withwithout}) now \emph{quantifies} it: of the $\approx-4.5$\,K
Siberian DJF bias vs ERA5, $\approx-3$\,K is the LPJG forest-cover collapse (via snow-albedo in spring \emph{and}
surface roughness in winter, \S\ref{sub:roughness}) and $\approx-1.4$\,K is a model-intrinsic over-strong stable-BL
inversion (\S\ref{sub:residual}). The clean run is \emph{better} than the Baseline globally (RMSD 1.514 vs 1.717\,K)
at \emph{near-unchanged} imbalance ($+0.2$\,W/m$^2$, within drift) --- indicating both are \emph{warming levers
largely orthogonal to} the TOA tuning. That reframes
the path forward:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Fix the LPJG boreal forest-cover bug (establishment limits \texttt{gdd5min\_est}/\texttt{twmin\_est} and/or
zero-LAI stem snow-masking --- see the companion note) and re-do the spin-up; this warms the NH high latitudes in
\emph{every} run, lowering the shared RMSD floor by $\approx$3\,K in the worst region.
\item Attack the residual with \emph{enhanced stable-BL mixing} in OpenIFS (\S\ref{sub:residual}) --- an
independent knob that relaxes the surface-trapped winter inversion.
\item On that warmer baseline, apply a \emph{moderate} mixing/coupling setting (06O-like, not 06V) for the
imbalance, plus the clean melt-pond changes --- the cooling it adds is then offset rather than additive.
\item Re-judge on the \emph{pattern} of CMPI change (\S\ref{sub:withwithout} method), not the global value, since
the PI-vs-PD offset is expected.
\end{enumerate}
In short: don't chase the imbalance with ever-colder atmospheres; remove the structural cold bias first, then the
moderate cooling needed for the imbalance becomes affordable.

\subsection{2026--07--24 coupled follow-up from the CRUNCEP3-initialized branch: the year-50 land--ocean split refines the path}\label{sub:cruncep3_init_followup}
After the earlier report structure was drafted, the coupled
CRUNCEP3-initialized branch (alias ``080a'') reached
50 years, which adds an important constraint on the tuning strategy. The new
result is \emph{not} a simple hemisphere-wide cold drift. By year 50 the system
shows a marked \textbf{warm-ocean / cold-land split}, while boreal tree cover
continues to retreat --- most strongly over Siberia / East Siberia.

Two points matter:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item The older LPJG story remains necessary: the spin-up / competition state is
still part of the problem, and the vegetation levers are real.
\item But the 50-year coupled adjustment shows that a \emph{regional continental-land}
climate failure also develops, so vegetation-only retuning is not sufficient.
\end{enumerate}

The finished year-50 diagnostics make the asymmetry explicit. Relative to year 0,
NH45+ \texttt{TREEFPC} declines from $0.336$ to $0.215$, but the strongest loss is
concentrated over Siberia / East Siberia. In the Siberian box (55--75\textdegree N,
60--180\textdegree E) \texttt{TREEFPC} drops by $-0.188$, and in East Siberia
(55--75\textdegree N, 90--160\textdegree E) by $-0.232$, accompanied by a strong
drop in \texttt{AGDD5} ($-89$ and $-133$ respectively). At the same time,
NH45+ aggregate \texttt{AGDD5} actually \emph{increases}, and Scandinavia / Canada
also warm in thermal-growing metrics. So the problem is not ``the NH is too cold'';
it is that \textbf{boreal continental land, especially East Siberia, remains too

\section{Work plan: a systematic path to a warm-floor, surface-balanced PI control}\label{sec:workplan}
\textbf{Two revised targets.} (i) net \emph{surface} energy balance $|F_\mathrm{sfc}|\lesssim0.3$\,W/m$^2$ ---
\emph{not} TOA. Surface and TOA net flux track to within $\approx0.1$\,W/m$^2$ in every run (\S\ref{sec:rad}); they
are one problem, so a surface-zero target is equivalent to TOA\,$\approx-0.1$\,W/m$^2$, marginally stricter than
TOA$=0$. (ii) global 2\,m-T bias vs ERA5 $\approx-1.3$\,\textdegree C --- the physically-expected PI-minus-present-day
offset, \emph{not} zero: a run that reaches $-1.3$\,\textdegree C is \emph{correct}, not cold-biased.

\textbf{Ordering principle.} The imbalance can only be cut by cooling, and every cooling lever over-cools if applied
to today's cold-biased base. So we \emph{first} raise the warm floor with two structural fixes that are
\emph{orthogonal to the TOA balance} (they change the land surface and BL mixing, not the radiation budget ---
\S\ref{sub:withwithout} showed the no-LPJG floor sits at \emph{near-unchanged} imbalance, $+0.2$\,W/m$^2$ within
drift), \emph{then} spend a
\emph{moderate} cooling lever to reach surface balance, now affordable because the floor is $\sim3$--$4$\,K warmer
where it was worst. Phases 1A/1B are independent (parallel); Phase 2 depends on both; Phase 3 gates.

\subsection{Phase 1A --- Restore boreal tree cover (LPJG establishment)}
\textbf{Literature \& observational basis --- what realistic trees require (so we don't green the tundra).} The
observed arctic/boreal treeline sits near the July $10$\,\textdegree C isotherm (K\"oppen), refined by K\"orner \&
Paulsen (2004) to a growing-season mean of $\sim6$--$7$\,\textdegree C over $\sim90$ days, with the northern boreal
limit at $\lesssim30$ days $>10$\,\textdegree C. \textbf{Crucially, LPJG's current gates are already the standard,
treeline-calibrated values} (Smith et al.\ 2001; Sitch et al.\ 2003 --- byte-identical to the canonical
\texttt{global.ins}): BNE/BINE \texttt{twmin\_est}$=5$ (already \emph{below} the $10$\,\textdegree C isotherm) and
\texttt{gdd5min\_est}$=500$ sit essentially \emph{at} the observed treeline --- so loosening them risks trees north
of the real treeline. Two further realism facts constrain the fix: (i) NE Siberia's forest is $\sim$80\,\% \emph{larch}
(deciduous \texttt{BNS}), because evergreen conifers cannot survive its extreme winters (Kharuk et al.; Herzschuh
2020, \emph{Glob.\ Ecol.\ Biogeogr.}\ 29, 198--206, the deciduous-vs-evergreen distribution paper) --- LPJG already
encodes this (BNE/BINE \texttt{tcmin\_surv}$=-31$ excludes them; BNS has \emph{no} cold gate and
\texttt{gdd5min\_est}$=350$), so the realistic tree there is larch, and it is \emph{already permitted}. (ii) Closed
taiga tree cover is $\sim0.4$--$0.7$ (MODIS VCF), falling below $0.2$ at the taiga--tundra ecotone --- the spatial
shape cvh must reproduce.

\textbf{Larch caveat.} The realistic NE-Siberian tree is \emph{deciduous} larch, whose leafless winter canopy masks
little snow --- so the spring \emph{albedo} mechanism is genuinely weaker over the larch zone (though it retains full
force over the evergreen west Eurasia / N.\ America). This only re-weights the two mechanisms, it does not soften the
conclusion: the \emph{roughness} mechanism survives (even leafless, larch is $\gg$ rougher than grass/tundra), and the
seasonality fingerprint (\S\ref{sub:seasonality}) shows the LPJG cold effect is co-equal in the \emph{sunless}
Nov--Jan months --- i.e.\ the dark-season roughness/inversion part is what carries the larch zone.

\textbf{The coupled winter cold is a self-inflicted trap, not the trigger.} At the cells where prescribed veg has
forest but LPJG does not (1785 boreal-land cells), the growing-season gates are already cleared in every run
(JJA $\ge12$\,\textdegree C, GDD5 $>500$); only the winter \texttt{tcmin\_est}$=-30$ evergreen gate binds. But against
the \emph{forested} WITHOUT-LPJG climate it excludes evergreens on just $23\%$ of cells ($\approx$ERA5's $22\%$),
vs $43\%$ in the degraded WITH-LPJG run --- the missing forest's own $-3.3$\,\textdegree C winter cooling doubles the
exclusion. So it is a \textbf{bistable trap} seeded by the under-forested spin-up, not a climate trigger.

\textbf{The decisive test settles it (\S\ref{sub:decisive}): competition, not climate} --- so Phase 1A's lever is the
\textbf{competition parameters}, not the establishment gates: boreal \texttt{greff\_min} (0.03 BNE / 0.09 BINE,BNS),
C3-grass competitiveness, and cold-tree photosynthesis \texttt{pstemp\_low}$=10$\,\textdegree C. Robustly, trees
\emph{do} establish (nonzero density) and fire is never involved (burnt fraction $=0$ despite BLAZE) --- the failure
is canopy-closure/productivity competition, not a gate or a fire problem. Phase-1B winter warming
(\S\ref{sub:phase1b}) is a \emph{secondary} aid, only on the coldest evergreen-gated cells.

\textbf{Why not just relax the gates?} The summer gates are not binding (above), so loosening them cannot help; and
the \emph{one} binding gate --- the winter \texttt{tcmin\_est} --- correctly excludes evergreens from the
extreme-cold larch zone, so relaxing it would plant \emph{unrealistic} evergreens where even ERA5 says larch. The
fix is therefore the winter \emph{climate}, not the limits. (Keep \texttt{iftreefracca 1}, \texttt{global.ins}:150:
cvh is the summed tree \emph{crown area}, so it tracks establishment success directly.)

\textbf{Screen \& deploy.} Tune the competition knobs on the cheap standalone LPJG spin-up (the OIFS--AMIP forcing of
\S\ref{sub:decisive}; minutes--hours of wall-time), targeting the MODIS-VCF pattern ($\sim0.4$--$0.7$ closed taiga,
$<0.2$ at the ecotone, larch winning the cold NE) and \dead{a closed canopy (FORESTFPC$>0$)}\footnote{\textbf{Void diagnostic.} \texttt{FORESTFPC} sums only PFTs with \texttt{landcover==FOREST} (\texttt{commonoutput.cpp}:1143), i.e.\ the \emph{managed-forest} land-use class; natural trees are counted in \texttt{TREEFPC} via the \texttt{NATURAL} branch. In this PI configuration nothing is allocated to FOREST (\texttt{Natural\_sum}$=0.684$, \texttt{Total}$=0.665$), so \texttt{FORESTFPC}$\equiv0$ \emph{by construction} while \texttt{TREEFPC}$=0.323$. It is a land-use bookkeeping artifact and says nothing about canopy closure.} --- tree NPP $>$ grass NPP ---
\emph{not} merely a higher global mean. On a passing screen, re-do the coupled LPJG spin-up, regenerate the restart,
and carry it into Phase 2.

\subsection{Execution: the clean AMIP--noLPJG forcing generator}\label{sub:amipgen}
The decisive test (\S\ref{sub:decisive}) needs a \emph{feedback-free} climate to drive it --- reusing coupled-LPJG
output would carry the very cold bias we are testing. We therefore built a dedicated \textbf{OpenIFS--AMIP} generator
at TCO95 (\texttt{oifsamip-cy48}, git-aligned to \texttt{awiesm3-develop}): AMIP prescribes observed SST/sea-ice, so
the atmosphere has no coupled ocean/LPJG feedback. Building and running it required fixing three esm-tools bugs (a
FESOM-less crash, missing XIOS/coupling compile flags, a missing WAM date-preprocessing recipe --- all committed \&
pushed; detail in App.~\ref{app:impl}). A 10-yr $\sim$PI decade (1870--79 observed SST, GHG fixed 1850) produced the
daily forcing. \emph{One methodological catch worth recording:} the first re-spin came out barren everywhere because
the XIOS field-def de-accumulated fluxes over the 6\,h \texttt{freq\_op} cadence instead of the true
\texttt{NFRHIS$\times$TSTEP}$=1$\,h period, making \texttt{rsns}/\texttt{rlns}/\texttt{pr} exactly 6$\times$ too low
(net SW $28$ vs $166$\,W/m$^2$) --- fixed by a validated $\times6$ patch and, at root, by templating the divisor from
esm-tools. \textbf{Expected payoff} of the whole Phase~1A (\S\ref{sub:levervsera5}): $\approx-3$\,K off the Siberian
DJF bias, $\approx-0.2$\,K global RMSD, at near-unchanged imbalance.

\subsection{Phase 1B --- Enhance stable-boundary-layer mixing (OpenIFS)}\label{sub:phase1b}
\textbf{Change.} The residual $\approx1.4$\,K surface-trapped inversion (\S\ref{sub:residual}) is relaxed by
strengthening stable-BL turbulent mixing in the EDMF K-diffusion scheme via the ``long-tail'' (LTG) stability
functions --- lengthening the tail keeps mixing non-zero at large Richardson number and weakens the over-strong
surface inversion. There is no namelist for the stable branch, so this is $\sim$2 single-line OpenIFS source edits
(minimum stable mixing length \texttt{ZLMIN} and the LTG coefficient \texttt{ZCB}) plus recompile; the exact
file:line targets and secondary companions are in App.~\ref{app:impl}. \emph{Bear the documented side-effects in
mind} (\S\ref{sub:residual}): long-tail mixing beyond the observational range can deepen cyclones and degrade low
cloud, so verify the storm-track/low-cloud climatology, and pre-test cloud phase / LW$\downarrow$ (Pithan et al.\
2014) since the residual may be partly a cloud-microphysics rather than turbulence error.

\textbf{Test.} Change \emph{one} knob first (\texttt{ZLMIN}) to keep attribution clean; scan 2--3 values with short
(1--5\,yr) coupled sensitivity runs of the no-LPJG reference. \textbf{Gate:} the DJFM low-level inversion bias
(\texttt{djf\_second\_driver.py}) moves from $+1.4$\,K toward ERA5's $+4.2$\,K level and the residual boreal cold
shrinks, \emph{without} eroding legitimate inversions or warming the tropics (judge globally via \texttt{part8}/CMPI
pattern). \textbf{Risk:} over-mixing warms too broadly / degrades low cloud --- mitigate with small steps and
global CMPI-pattern judging.

\section{The feedback-free AMIP reference (2026--07--27/28)}\label{sec:amip}

our coupled tree.
\item \textbf{Sea albedo.} The old runscript carried \texttt{TMPRALBSEAD}$=0.075$. The coupled runs never
set it, so they use the source default \texttt{RALBSEAD}$=0.06$ (\texttt{susrad\_mod.F90}:107); OASIS

$1$\,W/m$^2$ of reflected SW --- comparable to the quantity being attributed. Removed.
\end{itemize}

mismatch sits on the radiation axis. The direct evidence remains the forcing-transfer test
(\S\ref{sub:xfer}), which does not depend on which variable carries it.

\dead{Burning off that cloud stops reflecting it at TOA, costing $\approx+0.18$\,W/m$^2$ on the annual global
TOA --- a $27\,\%$ worsening --- so a pure cloud fix cannot meet a fixed-TOA constraint, and only a
radiatively neutral surface-flux lever can.} Two things falsify that framing:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{The target is the \emph{surface}, not TOA} (\S\ref{sub:snowenth}), and the drift it controls is
driven by the flux into the \emph{ocean}. The cloud error is over \emph{land}
(\S\ref{sub:ceres}), where annual-mean energy storage is $\approx0$ --- land returns what it receives. A
boreal-land cloud correction therefore barely touches the quantity that governs spin-up drift.
\item \textbf{The error is land-specific}, so the correction need not be a global knob at all
(\S\ref{sub:ceres}) --- which was the whole reason a TOA cost was incurred in the first estimate.
\end{itemize}
The two objectives are consequently far more separable than the original framing implied, and the cloud
error can be attacked at its diagnosed cause rather than through a proxy.

\subsection{The stomatal-resistance sensitivity test (experiment A)}\label{sub:expA}
A second, radiatively neutral lever was nonetheless identified and is being tested, because the measured
Bowen ratio over Siberian JJA land is $\mathrm{SH/LH}=-24.6/-56.8=\mathbf{0.43}$, low against the general
observed picture for boreal conifer forest. Minimum canopy resistance for boreal needleleaf
(\texttt{susveg\_mod.F90}:266--267) is $250$\,s/m, with $500$ retained just above as a comment.

\textbf{Provenance --- and a correction.} \dead{$500$ is the IFS default and has been halved in this build.}
Git refutes this: both the active $250$ and the commented $500$ are present in
commit \texttt{cf82033}, ``initial commit of 48r1 released version'', and no AWI commit has ever touched
\texttt{RVRSMIN}. \textbf{$250$ is what ECMWF ships in 48r1}; $500$ is the older HTESSEL-era value they
themselves revised. Setting $500$ moves \emph{away} from the released configuration, so this is a
\textbf{sensitivity test, not a default restoration}.

\emph{Status:} \texttt{amip\_expA\_rvrsmin500}, 6\,yr, paired against \texttt{amip\_pi\_base} on 1872--1875.
Verified compiled in by inspecting the rebuilt object file (the value appears twice for types 3--4; $250$
survives only for Desert and Mixed Forest). Note this tests \emph{partitioning}, not the diagnosed cause: if
the cloud error is real then a resistance change compensates for it, and with $11$\,W/m$^2$ less available
energy there is also less to repartition. \emph{Caveat:} ``Bowen $0.43$ is too low'' is inferred from the
literature's general picture, not measured --- neither CRUNCEP3 nor CERES supplies SH/LH here, and FLUXNET
boreal sites would settle it.

\textbf{A connection worth testing in the same run.} Excess summer evapotranspiration over boreal land would
\emph{both} cool the surface directly \emph{and} moisten the boundary layer, feeding the excess low cloud
(model \texttt{lcc}$=50.5\,\%$ over Siberian land in JJA). If cutting transpiration also reduces low cloud,
the partitioning error and the cloud error are the same problem, and experiment A tests both at once.

\section{Revised plan: tune the atmosphere on AMIP}\label{sec:amipplan}
Both errors that remain --- $\approx0.83$\,W/m$^2$ of TOA imbalance (\S\ref{sub:amiptoa}) and
$\approx2.2$\,K of Siberian summer cold (\S\ref{sub:amipsummer}) --- are atmospheric, and both are now
\emph{measured} in a configuration where neither the ocean nor LPJ-GUESS can confound the answer. They should
therefore be tuned on AMIP, not in coupled mode. This does not retire Phases~1A/1B of \S\ref{sec:workplan}
--- the LPJG competition fix and the stable-BL mixing fix still target the \emph{cold-season} bias --- but it
inserts a cheaper, better-posed stage ahead of Phase~2.

\textbf{Two objectives, now known to be largely separable} (\S\ref{sub:jjaknob}):
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Surface energy balance $\to0$} to cut spin-up drift. This is an \emph{ocean} surface-flux
problem; the relevant number is $F_\mathrm{sfc}$ over ocean, which has \emph{not} yet been computed --- all
figures quoted so far ($+0.52$ AMIP, $+0.95$ 09C, $+1.77$ 080a) are global. \textbf{Open item.}
\item \textbf{Boreal summer warmth} to restore GDD5 and let trees compete. This is a \emph{land} problem,
caused by an $11$\,W/m$^2$ cloud-driven SW deficit that is absent over ocean (\S\ref{sub:ceres}).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why AMIP is the right testbed.} $2.3$ node-hours per model year against $15.8$ coupled --- a

initialisation shock, so discard the first 1--2 years. For paired tuning experiments 5\,yr (1 discarded

\section{Round 10 design: a two-pronged, targeted attack (2026--07--28)}\label{sec:round10}
\emph{Original design as proposed. Operator notes to be appended in \S\ref{sub:r10notes}.}

\textbf{The structural fact the design rests on.} The two largest regional radiation errors have
\emph{opposite} sign at TOA (\S\ref{sub:sobands}, \S\ref{sub:ceres}), so fixing them together is
self-balancing, whereas fixing either alone is not:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l l l r}
\toprule
 & error & fixing it & $\Delta$TOA \\
\midrule
\textbf{A1 Southern Ocean} & too \emph{little} cloud ($-6.6$\,pp, SW CRE $+7.8$) & adds cloud, reflects more &
$\mathbf{-0.5}$ to $-0.6$ \\
\textbf{A2 boreal land} & too \emph{much} cloud ($+8.2$\,pp, surface SW $-11$ in JJA) & removes cloud, absorbs more &
$\mathbf{+0.1}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Prong A --- AMIP (atmosphere), A1 before A2}
\textbf{A1 first.} It moves the global target in the right direction ($+0.67\to\approx+0.1$) \emph{and}
attacks the error the campaign has suspected since \S\ref{sec:so}. A2 then costs back only $\approx0.1$ of the
$\approx0.55$ that A1 buys, so the boreal fix becomes affordable --- the ``warm floor'' logic of Part~III,
relocated from the vegetation to the atmosphere.

\textbf{Critical: in AMIP the Southern-Ocean target is \emph{radiation only}, never temperature.}
SST and sea ice are prescribed, so the SO surface temperature cannot respond to a radiation error --- the
excess absorbed shortwave is simply exported through the fixed boundary. \textbf{A1 must therefore be judged
on SW CRE and cloud area against CERES, and it is meaningless to look for an SO temperature response in
AMIP.} This is also why the SO \emph{warm} bias appears only in AWI-CM3/AWI-ESM3: it is a \emph{coupled}
phenomenon, in which the same $+5.8$\,W/m$^2$ of excess absorbed energy has somewhere to go and warms the
ocean. The causal chain is then explicit and closes the compensating-error argument of \S\ref{sec:so}:
\begin{center}\small
AMIP SO cloud deficit ($-6.6$\,pp) $\to$ $+5.8$\,W/m$^2$ excess absorbed $\to$
\emph{(coupled only)} SO warm SST bias $\to$ suppressed by the 06T/06V ocean-mixing levers
\end{center}
i.e.\ Part~I was damping Weddell/Ross convection to compensate an \emph{atmospheric} cloud error. It follows
that the only test of whether A1 fixed the real problem is a \emph{coupled} run --- AMIP can confirm the
radiation is right, but not that the SO warm bias is gone.

\textbf{On levers.} OpenIFS has no land-only cloud knob, and that is informative rather than obstructive: the
land-specificity of A2 ($-16.5$\,W/m$^2$ CRE error over Siberian land against $-2.2$ over ocean at the same
latitude) is itself evidence that the cause is \textbf{land-surface moisture supply}, not the cloud scheme ---
which makes the \texttt{RVRSMIN} sensitivity test (\S\ref{sub:expA}) potentially the actual fix rather than a
proxy for one. A1, being over ocean, is a genuine cloud-scheme / microphysics target.

\textbf{Guardrails on every A-run} --- all four, every time:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item global net TOA \emph{and} surface flux (state which convention, \S\ref{sub:snowenth});
\item SO 45--65\textdegree S SW CRE and cloud area against CERES --- \emph{radiation only}, since prescribed
SST makes an SO temperature response impossible in AMIP by construction (above);
\item \textbf{the tropics} --- $50\,\%$ of the globe and the largest single contribution ($-1.26$), and the
term the 06O/06T/06V levers degraded (\S\ref{sec:cmpi});
\item CMPI by \emph{pattern}, not the global number.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prong B --- LPJ-GUESS, and it must go second}
\textbf{Do not tune competition parameters yet.} The tree deficit is largely forcing-driven ($-54\,\%$
Siberian \texttt{TREEFPC} from the forcing swap alone, \S\ref{sub:xfer}), so tuning LPJG while its forcing is
$11$\,W/m$^2$ dim and $2.2$\,K cold would bake the atmospheric error into vegetation parameters. That is the
compensating-error trap, and it is what the 07-series was already doing --- which is the natural reading of
why it achieved nothing (\S\ref{sec:round09}).

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item Complete Prong A.
\item \textbf{Regenerate the LPJG forcing} from the corrected atmosphere. Enabling work required:
\texttt{amip\_pi\_base} uses the default \texttt{file\_def} and emits the evaluation fields but \emph{not} the
daily LPJG forcing set, so a merged \texttt{file\_def} (or a second short run) is needed.
\item \textbf{Re-spin and re-derive the residual.} Whatever tree deficit survives against a corrected forcing
is the real LPJG target. Only then are \texttt{greff\_min} / \texttt{pstemp\_low} legitimate levers.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Gates, replacing the void one.} \dead{FORESTFPC$>0$} is unachievable by construction
(\S\ref{sub:xfer}) and must not be used. Use instead: \texttt{TREEFPC} against the CRUNCEP-arm equilibrium
($0.357$ Siberia, $0.359$ E.\ Siberia); \texttt{AGDD5} recovery toward $776$/$748$; and \texttt{cvh} toward
the MODIS $0.4$--$0.7$ closed-taiga range.

\subsection{Ordering}
\textbf{A1 $\to$ A2 $\to$ regenerate forcing $\to$ re-spin LPJG $\to$ B.} Prong B cannot be evaluated honestly
until Prong A has fixed what feeds it.
