The Peat–LUH3 Conflict

Where the prescribed peat map and the LUH3 land-use dataset disagree, why no choice of reference year fixes it, what we changed, and the restart defect underneath it all that turns a 10-9 rounding difference into whole stand types switching on and off.

Jan Streffing · rev 2, 18 August 2026 · handover to Laszlo Hajdu

What changed since rev 1 (17 August)

Four things, two of which correct rev 1 rather than extend it. If you read the first version, read this list and then sections 3, 5 and 6.

  • The restart fix is implemented, not just proposed. Rev 1 ended by asking whether the restored frac should be trusted. It now is, behind restart_target_continuity (default on), in three commits. The question stands as a review request, but there is code to review.
  • The conflict population is mostly mid-latitude cropland, not Arctic rangeland. A live run reported seven distinct refusing cells; six are between 37 °N and 56 °N. Rev 1's reindeer-grazing explanation describes the one cell we had looked at, and does not generalise. See section 2.
  • peat_lu_conflict_policy 1 had never once executed. A Jinja trailing-newline bug silently ate the last line of the instruction template. Found and fixed 17 August. Rev 1 implied the switch was exercised; it was not.
  • The A/B measurement still has no number. Attempt 1 died in seventy seconds on two more roundoff tolerances — one of them in 46e7a84, so it is worth your eye specifically (section 7). Attempt 2 is running. The pass criterion is written down in advance, including what counts as a failed test — section 6.

1. The short version

Two prescribed inputs disagree about how much of a grid cell can be peatland, at about 0.7 % of cells. It is not an epoch mismatch and not a reference-year mistake — the disagreement survives every LUH3 year tested, and 1850 is the most favourable of them. Which dataset wins is now a run-time switch rather than a hard-coded constant, defaulting to LUH3 so an unmodified instruction file stays CMIP7-compliant.

Chasing a peat-area oscillation in a piControl where peat should be frozen led somewhere more general: the restart is not a state snapshot. The land-cover fractions are serialised correctly and then discarded on read, re-derived by summing stand areas, and fed to threshold tests that flip whole categories on a 10-9 difference. That is the root cause, it now has a fix, and it is the one thing here we would most like a second opinion on.

No carbon is lost — the oscillation is bookkeeping, not a leak. But it means restart-continuation is not bit-identical to straight-through simulation.

2. What the conflict actually is

Take the cell that first stopped a run, at 67.7917 °N 19.2857 °E in Arctic Sweden:

peat map prescribes          0.226400
physical stand in restart    0.191726
LUH3 NATURAL available       0.088604   <- pinned to 1850

The peat stand does not fit in the natural area LUH3 leaves for it. Two details were needed to reproduce that 0.088604 exactly: our configuration runs run_barren 0, so NATURAL also absorbs the LUH3 closure gap rather than being the raw primf+primn+secdf+secdn (which is 0.043008); and cells with no usable LUH3 state fall back to NATURAL = 1 rather than taking the ~2×1020 fill value into the arithmetic.

Two things follow that rev 1 got wrong or could not see.

The population is agricultural, not Arctic. Six of seven sit in the mid-latitude cropland belt. Whatever the general mechanism is, it is not reindeer — that explanation fits the one cell we happened to open. The honest generalisation is weaker and more useful: LUH3 assigns these cells almost entirely to crop and pasture, and our peat map says a substantial fraction of them is peat, and both can be true of the real world (drained peat farmland is ordinary) while being mutually exclusive in a land-cover accounting where peat is carved only from NATURAL.

At two cells, policy 0 does not cap peat — it annihilates it. NATURAL is exactly 0 in Tatarstan and 8.4×10-9 in Korea. There is no fraction to cap into. A cap-and-transfer would move the entire peat stand and its pools out, permanently, on the first land-use year. That is a substantive argument for the refusal being right rather than merely conservative, and it was not available when rev 1 was written.

Why the epoch explanation is wrong

LUH3 does vary with epoch: at the Lapland cell it reconstructs a rangeland maximum that climbs from 850 onwards, peaks in 1910, and then vanishes abruptly in the 1950s.

 850   range 0.2115   NATURAL 0.7885
1300   range 0.5651   NATURAL 0.4349
1850   range 0.9114   NATURAL 0.0886   <- piControl reference year
1910   range 0.9433   NATURAL 0.0567   <- true maximum
1950   range 0.8958   NATURAL 0.1042
1955   range 0.4925   NATURAL 0.5075   <- collapses in one decade
1960   range 0.0892   NATURAL 0.9108
2000   range 0.0240   NATURAL 0.9760

Two things in that series are worth a second look. NATURAL first rises back above the 0.1917 physical peat stand in 1952 — so on this cell the conflict exists for the entire pre-1952 period and disappears afterwards. And the collapse is a step, not a decline: rangeland falls by a factor of ten between 1950 and 1960. Land-use history does not move like that, so this looks like a change of source inventory inside the LUH3 reconstruction rather than anything that happened in Lapland.

So it is tempting to blame the 1850 pin. That is wrong on both counts. 1850 is correct and required — 1850 forcing is the piControl convention, and our model years 1350–1399 are arbitrary spin-up labels, not real dates. Reading LUH3 at the calendar year would be a bug, not a fix.

And 1850 is also the best year available. Counting cells where the peat map exceeds the LUH3 natural area:

Conflicts grow toward the present because cropland and pasture expanded globally, squeezing NATURAL everywhere. 1850 leaves the most room of any epoch tested, and 62 of the 73 cells that conflict at 1850 also conflict at 2000.

The conclusion is therefore the plainer one: the peat map and LUH3 are two independent datasets that disagree at about 0.7 % of cells, and no choice of epoch makes that go away. That is why a policy switch is the right response rather than a reference-year fix.

Struck from rev 1: "LUH3 range at 68 °N is extensive reindeer grazing, which in reality happens on peatland." True of the Lapland cell, and it may still bear on whether range should map to PASTURE at high latitude — but six of the seven observed cells are mid-latitude cropland, so it is not the root cause of the conflict as a whole.

Worth knowing: neither CMIP6 predecessor of this configuration could hit this. EC-Earth3's LPJ-GUESS reads PEATLAND from the land-use file itself, so there is no second dataset to disagree with — its file_peat is a dead declaration, referenced nowhere — and its coupled setup runs run_landcover 0 regardless. JSBACH3 has no peatland type at all.

3. The decision

A new instruction-file switch, peat_lu_conflict_policy, default 0.

PolicyBehaviourUse for
0
default		 LUH3 authoritative. Peat is capped to the available NATURAL and the surplus area and its pools are moved by landcover_dynamics. Under fixed_LU that machinery never runs, so the policy refuses with an explanatory error rather than truncating silently. CMIP7, historical, scenario — anywhere land use is an applied common forcing that must be reproduced exactly.
1 Physical peat authoritative. The stand keeps its area and the non-peat categories are scaled into the remainder. Nothing is discarded, so no transfer is needed and it is safe in every code path. Fixed-forcing control runs only.

The case for allowing policy 1 at all: north of 60 °N, mean LUH3 rangeland at 1850 is 0.0040, while northern peat holds on the order of 500–1000 PgC and sets the soil thermal regime. In a pre-industrial control, boreal land use is noise and peat is signal. In a historical run that reasoning inverts, which is exactly why 0 is the default.

Why policy 0 refuses instead of capping: an earlier attempt capped anyway. It rewrote the bookkeeping while the physical stand kept its area, and was caught one call later by the very next check — st=2 metadata=0.0886 physical=0.1917. Laszlo's comment that capping "would discard state without a transfer" is structurally true in the fixed_LU path, not a stylistic preference. The two zero-NATURAL cells in section 2 sharpen this: there, capping is not lossy, it is total.

Correction to rev 1. The switch is real but had never executed when rev 1 was published. Jinja strips the trailing newline on render, so a parameter placed on the last line of a .ins template is silently dropped — and peat_lu_conflict_policy was the last line. Every run that was supposed to test policy 1 ran policy 0 instead and died on the refusal. Fixed 17 August by keeping ! end as the final line. If you write instruction templates, this will bite you too.

4. Peat that moved when it should not have

Checking AWI-ESM3-VEG-HR-CMIP7-piControl (a270089), which runs fixed_LU: 1850 and so should hold peat perfectly still across all 97 legs:

  yr    cells   peat>0   mean PEAT_STANDFRAC     SoilC
1851   107132    47296   0.042686               8.386
1887   107132    44281   0.040026               8.454
1959   107132    48049   0.043379               8.514
2007   107132    43467   0.039192               8.551
2043   107132    44361   0.039904               8.572

The peat-bearing cell count swings between 43 467 and 48 049 — about ten percent — with no trend and full recovery. Total cell count is constant, so it is not a grid artefact. Crop area is flat at 0.6062 across all 194 years, so fixed_LU is working and LUH3 is not evolving.

The diagnostic signature is the tell: the value is identical across both years within a leg and steps at leg boundaries. A physical process would vary year to year. This is peat being re-derived at each restart and landing somewhere slightly different.

The carbon does not follow it. Soil carbon varies only 2.2 % and rises monotonically — ordinary spin-up accumulation. If 4 600 peat stands were being destroyed and recreated, soil carbon would dip and recover by several percent. It does not. So the stands are being re-counted, not destroyed.

Caveat: cpool.out is a gridcell total, so a peat→natural transfer within a cell conserves gridcell carbon and would be invisible in this check. Carbon leaving the land is ruled out; redistribution between stands inside a cell is not.

For contrast, the same diagnostic on our own arm — also fixed_LU: 1850, five legs, 50 years:

1359   10610 cells   4449 peat   0.041567
1369   10610         4449        0.041567
1379   10610         4449        0.041567
1389   10610         4449        0.041567
1399   10610         4449        0.041567

Identical to six decimal places. The discriminator between the two runs is still open — the obvious candidates are resolution (107 132 cells against 10 610, so many more marginal cells near whatever threshold creates or kills a peat stand) and the v3.4.2 binary lineage. That binary carries none of the newer land-cover checks.

5. What drives the oscillation: marginal cells amplify tiny land-use noise

Land use is not quite constant in these runs even though fixed_LU: 1850 pins it. Comparing the land-use tile fractions leg by leg, in both the HR piControl and Spinup_cont3:

       crp          peat cells
  4.161630              48049   <- identical baseline in BOTH runs
  4.163668              47296
  4.165642              46513
  4.172533              44281
  4.175385              43467

The relationship is monotonic and steep: a 0.33 % wobble in cropland area drives a 9.5 % swing in peat-bearing cells — roughly thirty-fold amplification. Both runs return repeatedly to exactly the same clean state (crp 4.161630, 48 049 peat cells) and make excursions from it.

That amplification is the peat–LUH3 conflict, seen at scale. Thousands of HR cells sit right at the boundary where peat only just fits inside the available NATURAL, so any small land-use perturbation tips them out — and back again when it reverses. Our TCO95 arm shows none of it because only a handful of cells are anywhere near that boundary.

A separate bug, found while chasing this, that is not the cause. The fixed_LU freeze at framework.cpp:4450 reads thisyear >= fixedLUafter, so gross land transfers are only switched off once the model calendar reaches the pinned year. Spin-ups numbered below it never trigger it: Spinup_cont3 runs 1650–1679 against fixed_LU 1850 and 0 of its 64 ranks report the freeze, so gross transitions stay on for the whole run. Our own 11I (1350–1399) has the same gap. It is worth fixing on its own merits — but the HR piControl runs 1850+, does fire the freeze, and still shows the same wobble and the same peat oscillation, so this is not what causes it.

6. The root cause: the restart discards what it just restored

The obvious question is why a restart perturbs land use at all — LPJ-GUESS writes a binary dump and reads it back, so continuation ought to be exact. It nearly is. Stand::serialize writes the pools, and both Gridcellst::serialize and Landcover::serialize write frac into the archive. The round trip is faithful; Stand::clone even uses it as a copy mechanism.

Then, on every restart, getlandcover throws the restored bookkeeping away and recomputes it:

physical_st[stand.stid]      += area;   // re-sum the restored stands
physical_lc[stand.landcover] += area;
physical_total               += area;
...
lc.frac[i] = physical_lc[i];            // overwrite what the dump restored
gcst.frac  = physical_st[i];

The code's own comment names the consequence: "physical_total is a sum over every stand in the cell, so it carries whatever double [roundoff]". Mid-leg, gcst.frac is the value carried in memory from the previous year; after a restart it is a fresh summation over stands. The two differ by 3.3×10-9 to 4.2×10-7 — precisely the drift the 1e-9 check was flagging.

That difference then meets threshold tests:

if (!is_cold_start && gcst.frac_old == 0.0 && new_frac > 0.0
        && new_frac < NEW_ST_THRESHOLD) {
    filtered_amount[st.landcover] += new_frac;
    gcst.frac = 0.0;                    // category dropped entirely
}
...
if (raw_luh3_crop_sum > lc.frac[CROPLAND])
    crop_fraction_scale = lc.frac[CROPLAND] / raw_luh3_crop_sum;

An equality test against zero and a comparison against a threshold, both evaluated on a quantity whose last digits depend on whether you arrived by restart or by simulation. Land the wrong side of one and a whole stand type switches on or off — a discrete jump produced by a 10-9 input.

That accounts for every property we measured: constant within a leg, stepping at leg boundaries, flipping between a small set of discrete states rather than drifting, and returning repeatedly to exactly the same baseline.

The fix, as built

Rev 1 stopped here and asked the question. It is now implemented, in three commits, behind restart_target_continuity (bool, default true):

commitwhat it does
8c068f3Repair-first land-cover checks, and the peat_lu_conflict_policy switch.
ab45fddDifference the land-cover target against the carried value rather than a fresh re-sum of the stands. This is the actual fix: the threshold tests stop seeing a summation artefact as a land-use change.
29ae612Latch the carried fractions per stand type, and match the check's summation order.

The latch is Gridcellst::frac_prev / frac_prev_year — per year, at stand-type granularity, and deliberately not serialised. It is a within-run continuity device, not new restart state, so it adds nothing to the archive layout. That matters because ArchiveStream is a raw byte stream — transfer(char*, streamsize), no magic number, no version, no field names — and any layout change reads old dumps as garbage.

Two implementation bugs, both worth knowing about because both were silent-ish:

  1. A half-applied version of the patch produced LUH3 previous LC/ST mismatch at 2.5×10-11 — small enough to look like noise, actually a missing half.
  2. The latch initially aggregated in a different order from the check that consumes it. Floating-point addition is not associative, so the two disagreed by ~3×10-9 against a 10-12 tolerance and killed both arms. The fix is that the latch now walks stlist in exactly the order the check does. If you touch this code, preserve that: the correctness of the comparison depends on the summation order matching, not merely on the values being mathematically equal.

Corrections to rev 1's analysis

A review caught two claims in the first design note that were wrong. Both are load-bearing for anyone re-deriving this:

  • landcover_reconcile_restart_state is not unreachable. Rev 1 claimed the end-of-year reconciliation is never hit in no-change years, so the invariant is never re-established. False — it forwards to synchronize_carried_landcover_state at landcover.cpp:129 and runs every year at :161.
  • Removing the first getlandcover pass is hygiene, not a fix. getlandcover runs twice per year under fixed_LU — via landcover_init and again via lc_changed. It is tempting to delete pass 1. But the defect is entirely in pass 2, which is unconditional, because all_fracs_const = false for LUH3 and the guard therefore always admits. We briefly recommended this as "the smallest true fix" and were wrong.

The measurement, and what would falsify it attempt 2 running

Two runs, one binary, one namelist line apart — restart_target_continuity 1 against 0, under fixed_LU, with print_lc_change_diag 1 emitting an LCDIAG line whenever a land-cover change is registered.

required:   LCDIAG count == 0  with the switch on
            LCDIAG count  > 0  with the switch off

If both arms come out zero the test proved nothing and must be redesigned. That is a failure, not a pass — recorded here in advance so it cannot be reinterpreted later.

Attempt 1 (18 August) produced no measurement at all, which is weaker still than the double-zero case. Both arms reported LCDIAG = 0, but every fpc.out and cpool.out was zero bytes: neither arm completed a model year, so the counter never reached the code that increments it. The two tolerances in section 7 were the cause. A monitor keyed on Slurm job state called this a completed run, because esm_tools reports COMPLETED on a crashed leg and advances the date file — one arm walked forward two legs on restarts that were never written. Anything watching these runs has to key on non-zero annual output, not job state. Attempt 2 is running on three legs. Nothing downstream should be merged until it reports.

7. Restart-precision repairs, in the same change

Three bookkeeping checks were converted from assert-first to repair-first, following what both CMIP6 predecessors did — EC-Earth3 rescales fractions with a warning; JSBACH3 iterates toward closure and only aborts if repair fails.

  • Stand-sum roundoff. lc_bookkeeping_tol() is INPUT_RESOLUTION × 1e-3 = 1e-9, scaled off the precision of the land-use input but applied to a sum of restart-state floats whose error floor is float epsilon. Measured worst case 4.2×10-7, about 3.5× float32 eps — no restart can pass it. Now renormalised below LC_PHYSICAL_SUM_REPAIR_LIMIT, fatal above.
  • Metadata against physical stands. Repaired at any magnitude with a logged warning; only non-finite or out-of-range is fatal. This direction cannot change a stock — the physical stands own the pools and the repair is literally gcst.frac = st_sum[s].

Measured on a one-day TCO95 restart once relaxed: 1099 renormalisations and 14 909 metadata repairs across a ~10 200-cell gridlist. Essentially the whole domain fails the 1e-9 check, not the twelve cells that happened to abort first.

Two more of the same, missed on the first pass (18 August)

Relaxing the checks in landcover.cpp left two in externalinput.cpp untouched, and those killed the first A/B attempt in about seventy seconds without either arm simulating a year. Same defect class, one level down.

The first is ours and the miss was ours too: 1e-6 was fitted to a measured worst case of 4.2e-7, and this configuration reaches twenty-two times that. It also sat an order of magnitude below LC_PHYSICAL_SUM_REPAIR_LIMIT, which guards the same quantity one call later — and even that 1e-5 cleared the observed maximum by only 7 %. Both are now 1e-4, picked structurally rather than fitted to the new maximum, so the constant does not need re-tuning the next time a configuration with more stands per cell appears.

The second one is in your code, and the message is the problem as much as the tolerance. close_luh3_base_fractions aborts with "available NATURAL area is insufficient", which sends you looking for a land-use conflict. The actual condition is that the LUH3 categories sum to 1 + 4.4e-8 by input roundoff at a cell whose NATURAL is already zero — the Tatarstan and Korea rows in the seven-cell table above. Genuine insufficiency is macroscopic and should still fail; this is not that. The repair rescales every category by 1/sum. Clamping the buffer to zero does not work: the sum would still be 1 + 4.4e-8 and would then fail the identity check on the next line. Commit c226bbf — please check we have not weakened something you put there deliberately.

9. Two environment traps that each cost a day

Not science, but they will cost you the same day they cost us.

  • /work drops 1–2.5 % of small-file writes. Measured 2/200 on ab0246, 5/200 on bb1469, 0/2000 on /scratch. Coarse I/O never notices, but a ~5000-file esm_runscripts staging copy essentially always loses something. When what it loses is the staged guess.ins, LPJ-GUESS reports "Bad instruction file!" and every rank exits 99 — which sends you hunting for a syntax error that is not there. We were wrong about the cause twice before measuring. Run on /scratch.
  • CMake fails on /work with missing link.txt / compiler_depend.ts — the same dropped-write fault, and a build hammers exactly that access pattern. Build with the object tree on /tmp.
